# GC VP A (Texas general contractor) - permit review slowed from weeks to months, low-bid retail

Note: the original is rough interviewer notes taken during the conversation; contact details and referral names removed.

## Role

Responsible "cradle to grave": business development (calling developers, discussing market and upcoming projects), conceptual pricing, helping manage costs in preconstruction, steering owners with their architect/engineer, sometimes working with the city, developing contract scope and language and finalizing with legal, project startup logs and set-up before mobilizing, ensuring schedule accuracy, handing the estimate to field teams, running or facilitating buyout, developing subs and scope, then managing the warranty period and starting over. Project management is standardized across jobs. "I manage the people that manage the work."

## Schedules and delays

- Schedule treated as a living document; change orders alter it. Roughly 75% of jobs finish on time, ~25% run some overtime, and ~85% of deviations have a reason such as document changes. Major events (e.g., hurricanes) can cause six months or ~$1M of damage.
- Consistent delay sources: permitting, weather, materials. Permitting went from 3-4 weeks to 3-4 months because fewer people review plans; apartment development has exploded. Example material delay: a door supplier was due to deliver last week with no new date, forcing temporary doors (cost and time, then removal).
- Labor: many Spanish-speaking workers; when immigration enforcement is in town work slows because people worry. War-driven diesel prices raise costs for trucked materials, pushing up concrete and steel; steel suppliers sometimes email notices of 15% price increases.
- Owners want faster projects (they pay loan interest); buildings that took 12 months now take 14-15 months of effective scope, so he pays a premium for staff who can use advanced software.

## Subs, bids and winning work

- Subs are "always an issue", often over money: the GC pays subs only after the owner pays the GC ("we aren't a bank"). Selection: safety record and a prequalification package documenting they pay bills, vendors and employees, with no lawsuits, accidents or fatalities.
- Rule of thumb: three bids per trade, to gauge the number and spot outliers. Never rely on one sub. Subs are chosen on who you know; a low-bidding plumber may be busy.
- Retail: for grocery/retail chains with 3-4 bidding contractors, ~99% go to the lowest bidder; ~85% of retail work comes from designer/owner packages, then bid, with occasional design partnerships. Owner relationships come from word of mouth, lunches, being relevant; the company got into one regional grocery chain after its first store came in under budget and two weeks early. Calls years later from people who remember you.
- Buyout: PM handles buyout of materials; for structural steel he hires a supplier and gives full info; the supplier is responsible.
- Sub loyalty anecdote: a sub that worked only for a key grocery client would put that client first over everyone, and a bad team player is a liability; "the GC knows a little about a lot."

## Tools and tech

- Procore for document control, photo documentation, accounting, meeting agendas/minutes, coordination; shifting everything onto it to be integrated. Older project-management tools became outdated. Salesforce for business development, a dedicated scheduling tool, Microsoft Office; "everyone loves Excel for tracking." Some clients use their own tools (Smartsheet, school-district systems).
- Starting to look at AI estimating software; evaluating takes time and money.
- Market moving toward AI and new equipment but many people and equipment are old-school; lots of technology available for estimating, document control, PM, accounting, scheduling, "lots of technology and not a lot of users because they are old people who won't change."
- Meeting minutes and photos serve as legal documents; RFIs and change approvals are tracked by cost.
