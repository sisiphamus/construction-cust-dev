# Electrical Sub Owner A (small Texas electrical contractor) - price pressure, bidding apps, GC distrust

## Business

- 4-6 electricians; service, commercial and industrial (manufacturing, metalworking, factories). Uses a bidding/lead platform to find projects and employs an estimator. Pays his crew hourly and tries to keep them working so he does not lose the team when scaling.

## Pains

- **Price is the biggest issue ("THE PRICES").** Wire and material costs climbing while GCs try to get work done at old prices; subs carry the higher expenses. Working under a GC means he cannot make the same amount; he is better compensated without GCs, especially in residential, and wants to focus on being just an electrical contractor.
- **GCs are unregulated.** "Tomorrow you could call yourself a GC": no license. People want things cheap and GCs get shady trying to skim costs. A good GC only exists with regulation; otherwise "work a project for 3 months and only get a few grand."
- **Draw structure (example, $10K job):** first draw at rough-in ~$5K, trim-out ~$2.5K, final ~$2.5K. If the paperwork is right there is no issue with financing.
- **Bidding apps drive competition.** Bidding platforms let everyone bid, so margins are set at ~10% (a $50K job nets ~$5K). "So many electricians in Texas." Apps also show how often GCs hire, so he tries to build relationships with GCs. In the Rio Grande Valley fewer electricians work, so there is more of a lock on the market.
- **Change orders** are what make money on locked contracts: all profit.
- **Marketing is now mandatory.** To succeed as a new contractor in a big city you need a marketing fund, watch reviews and spend thousands per month on ads. Older contractors are already "in the game."
- Unregulated areas of construction create housing issues; he cited per-square-foot rates rising along with wire prices.
