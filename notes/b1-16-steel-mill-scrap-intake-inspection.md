# Scrap Inspector A (large US steel mill) - intake inspection and material tracking

## Work
- Bulk inspects scrap in the yard, flying drones and driving around looking for problem material. Scrap is offloaded through a bulk terminal operator, which is allowed a certain amount of downtime per week. A lot of the scrap comes from Texas.
- Produces weekly and monthly scrap reports in Excel: what is on the ground, weeks of supply of each material, what is in transit versus newly bought. Example: bought about 120k tons for the next month from a single supplier.
- Reporting used to take about 8 hours; recent automation cut it to about 35 minutes, but he does not trust it yet and spent two weeks checking it.

## Pains
- Biggest issue: non-ferrous material and unsealed containers arriving in scrap from about 50 yards, some very large. If unsealed material reaches the furnace it can explode, break equipment and kill people.
- Visibility: drones only show the top layer; he cannot see underneath. Would like to scan everything somehow.
- Traceability: piles of 10k-19k tons must be zeroed out every six months; if unsealed material turns up at that point it cannot be traced to a source.

## Mitigation today
- Four operators who offload trucks and barges know what to look for. Fines of $25-30k on scrapyards for bad loads have improved quality ("not as bad as it used to be").

## Relevance
- Material tracking, source traceability and manual spreadsheet reporting are the opportunity areas. No buy signal.
