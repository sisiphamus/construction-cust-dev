# Sub Owner B (Midwest industrial sheet-metal fabricator) - coordination breakdown is the biggest pain

## Business
- Sheet-metal ductwork, mostly industrial (soybean and food-processing plants), four shops in two Midwest locations, ~200 employees. The industry is growing so fast "they just can't keep up." Currently building plants across the US and now Europe for a huge agribusiness owner. He describes himself as a "make it happen" guy rather than a salesman.
- Parts are cut, formed and welded. One recent plant needed about 50 people to produce the volume. Expanding equipment: lasers (~$1M each, "cheap"), plasma cutters.

## Pains
1. Materials: mills control flow and are hard to contact; sheet comes in coils that need special equipment to process, so he buys from distributors who process it, but distributors struggle to get enough. He waits weeks. He just hired someone to drive around to get a foot in the door with mills. "It's a struggle to scale."
2. Labor: few people want trades; when he graduated everyone went to college (he did not and "makes more than all of them"). Many retirements over 5-6 years not replaced. Data centers are being built everywhere and absorbing ~500 workers each.
3. Communication and coordination (biggest): keeping four shops, 15 people on every email, and hundreds on a project aligned. "Even with all the tech something always gets left out or built twice."
   - Each trade (HVAC, roofer, plumber) does its own CAD and communicates but is not on the same page; the job becomes a race to get work in before another trade's plan competes. Because he fabricates custom pieces, a conflict can mean scrapping ~$15k of material.
   - On very large jobs, even the engineering firm sends different plan versions to different parties.
   - Collision reports exist but often just list collisions; fixing them falls to the field.
   - Many layers (workers, small-group and big-group managers, office staff, engineers); thousands touch big industrial jobs. Hard to know who is working where (can the electricians get in with 10 lifts in there?).
   - GCs: "too many hands in the cookie pot"; the GC should order trades around but rarely does and often never came from a trade. GCs get swapped when things go wrong, so none see a job through. Wants a GC there from the beginning to the end.
   - Current tools: Teams, email, handwritten notes, and an Autodesk common-data-environment site where one person per trade at the owner manually uploads/updates contracts and schematics so there are records in court.
4. Data centers take ~3 years to build and the GPUs are outdated by then; speed is money.

## Ideas he floated
- Software that sorts and overlays every trade's layouts into one cohesive layout (he already does GPS layout and 3D scans of buildings, but nobody merges them).
- Projecting overlaid 2D plans onto a wall, turning them into 3D.
- Engineers should have to build what they design once.
- Advice to the interviewers: "Go to an area, find a problem, solve it."

## Buy signal
None; interest in the problem but no commitment. Follow-up calls ongoing.
