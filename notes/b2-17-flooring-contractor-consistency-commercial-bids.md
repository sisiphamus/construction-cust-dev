# Flooring Sub A (small flooring crew) - consistent workflow and commercial bid pressure

## Tools and workflow

- QuickBooks for estimates, invoices and receipts, all by email; "super easy." Unanswered calls go to voicemail.

## Main pain

- Staying consistent: continuous workflow and steady weekly income for his crews.
- Moving toward commercial: residential pays well per job but has many competitors; he matches or undercuts other quotes by a few hundred dollars, but on commercial properties bids get so low he cannot knock off $1,500 and "the math just isn't adding up," so he does not know how others bid so low.
- Commercial pays net-15/net-30; residential pays when the job is done. For commercial materials, he submits labor and materials pricing up front and once approved the customer funds materials.

## Positioning

- Great reviews, strong customer service, insured, one-year guarantee on work; gives past-client phone numbers as references; points prospects to Google reviews, which have won jobs by themselves.
- Residential customers always get multiple quotes; he offers to work with price if his bid is high. Takes jobs from ~250 to 3,500 sq ft, prefers large ones, charges separately for haul-off, rip-out and moving furniture.
- Tells customers to call or text any time, even 9pm.
- New employees find him via Google; he offers a one-week trial then moves them to his normal rate.
- Has had no issue with GCs (some are difficult); just landed a big commercial job, which keeps his crew busy. Commercial has the best money.
