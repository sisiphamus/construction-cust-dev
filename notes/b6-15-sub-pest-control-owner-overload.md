# Pest Control Owner A (small pest control company) - owner-operator admin overload

- Day-to-day is nonstop. He and his partner split HR, sales, QA, compliance and fleet maintenance. Once a company reaches 7-8 vehicles "you are not the little guy anymore" and revenue lets you hire quality help; they expect to reach that by year-end.
- Call load: 50-70 phone calls a day plus emails from job sites, peak season April to October; roughly half in the off season. He prefers to handle things as they come rather than call back; customers' main complaint about growing companies is "once a company got big I could never reach them."
- Quality of work is the #1 driver of reputation and referrals; scheduling slips are forgiven more easily.
- Hiring: one person per vehicle, six full-time, recruited via job boards. Resumes do not help; he screens by answers to personal questions and longevity at prior employers, prefers family-oriented people, and hires people brand new to the trade because experienced hires say "I used to do it this way." Observes young people are less inclined toward physical work.
- Contracts run year-round; spring brings bees, mosquitos and rodents. Food warehouses get roaches arriving in imported produce packaging; food businesses are mandated to have pest control.
- Gets 3-4 solicitation emails per week from investors/acquirers, increasing since COVID.
- Sees AI taking his type of work only in 10-20 years; believes the tech-industry boom is ending.
