# Permit Coordinator A (2-person Washington State permit firm) - drainage rejections and ineffective review software

## Business
- Two-person firm serving builders: runs plan sets from builders to reviewers, coordinates civil engineers, geotechnical engineers and other specialists, and handles approvals and procedures for zoning, land use and building code.

## Jurisdiction variation
- Counties in the state typically adopt the same base codes, but amendments, customization and interpretation vary by jurisdiction.
- Interpretation latitude varies by discipline: architects have less flexibility; civil and geotechnical engineers have more room to negotiate and propose alternatives.

## Pains and observations
- Drainage is the single most common reason plans are rejected in the state, and a blind spot in AI software.
- Relationships with permit staff matter, but high turnover (people changing roles and jurisdictions) loses historical knowledge and forces rebuilding rapport.
- Pre-development meetings vary: some jurisdictions help design toward approval; others are "just here to say no."
- Best practice: know what the jurisdiction wants to see before submitting, come with civil engineer plans, build staff relationships, and coordinate civil, geotechnical and MEP early.

## Existing software complaint
- The e-review platform used by their large home county (and a similar vendor product) does administrative work only: page orientation and technical correctness such as page numbering. It does not check code compliance or interpret building, zoning or land-use code, and the UX is poor. Result: busy work and permitting that is harder, not easier; users still do the real work manually.

## Assessment
- Gap for real code-checking and jurisdiction-specific requirement mapping, especially drainage. No buy signal.
