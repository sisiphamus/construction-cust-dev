# Developer A (large Florida-based commercial developer) - development is local

- Large commercial ($100M+) projects have more people and a managed process, so permitting feels less "physical". Smaller municipalities and residential projects are hit harder.
- Delay depends on municipal politics (pro-development vs anti) and staffing; some jurisdictions are chronically understaffed, others outsource plan review to third parties who are incentivized to be thorough (heavy-handed) to justify fees.
- Entering a new market: assemble the right local network, with local engineers and architects who have done about 50 projects in that municipality. "Development is a local business, period." No substitute for market experience; the path is diligence, keep pushing, adapt to surprises.
- In Houston he rarely uses code consultants because the regulatory environment is lighter, but recommends specialist code consultants for complex or regulated types (hospitals, schools, data centers).
