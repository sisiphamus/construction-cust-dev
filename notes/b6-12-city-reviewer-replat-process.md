# City Plat Reviewer A (large Texas city planning department) - replat process walkthrough

Single-channel recording; interviewers were Interviewer 1 and Interviewer 2. About 17 minutes.

## When a replat is needed
To put a use on land, subdivide it, or change land use. Lots restricted to single-family must be replatted to become commercial.

## Stage 1: check-in
- Applicant drafts and uploads all documents to a portal, then submits during an open window: opens Friday 6am, closes Monday 11am, every other week.
- Required: existing survey, title report, the drawing, correct GIS coordinates, everything current.
- Staff then check completeness. Missing or expired documents or unpaid fees produce an incomplete letter; applicant has until the next cycle. Deadline is two cycles (~4 weeks) or the application is inactivated. A fix right after closing (Mon-Tue) can still be accepted and reviewed.

## Stage 2: review
- A different planner takes over. Assignments go out Wednesday morning, reviews due end of day Tuesday.
- Checks code compliance against the subdivision chapter of the city code, building lines, typos and details. Example: lots under the 3,500 sqft minimum have options (50% building coverage, compensating open space, or an open-space amenities plan in a floodplain).
- Fast path: submit Monday and all good -> letter that it is checked in; if nothing is wrong you hear nothing until it is recommended for approval at the planning commission (meets Thursdays) the following week.

## Stage 3: recordation
Applicant books an appointment, pays fees, prints the final plat, and a recordation planner confirms all review markups were addressed. City signs and stamps; each Wednesday plats go to the county clerk (or county engineer if outside city limits).

## Failure modes
- #1: fees not paid (the client has not paid the applicant, or they forget). Then expired or missing documents (e.g., a drainage plan that takes long to obtain).
- Who fails: small and first-time applicants who do not know the process. Restarting costs only the fees already paid.
- Multi-agency: the county and other agencies also leave comments; if any agency disapproves, the project is deferred even if others approve.
- Reviewer load: ~12 projects per cycle all due Wed-to-Tue. Most stressful: big cycles on a fixed clock and unresponsive applicants whose work rolls over.

## Read
Planning/platting leg of the permitting machine. Pain is process navigation, document completeness and fee/coordination discipline, concentrated in small or infrequent users, which aligns with a first-pass-approval or permitting-as-a-service wedge. No buy signal.
