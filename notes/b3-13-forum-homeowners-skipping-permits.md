# Homeowner forum thread - Houston homeowners on skipping permits for a deck

## Synthesis

**Source:** A public regional forum thread (2025), shared by Interviewer 2 as a useful homeowner-sentiment resource. The original poster was also noted as a hot lead for permitting outreach.

**The post:** A Houston homeowner is about to rebuild an old deck (explicitly >30" high and >150 sq ft, above the usual permit-exempt thresholds). All the lumber has already arrived and he only just realized he has not thought about permits. He asks about building without one.

**What the crowd said (the real signal):**

- **Dominant sentiment: just build it, skip the permit,** especially in unincorporated county. "If it's unincorporated just shut up and build it. If you're inside city limits that demand a permit, shut up and build it even faster."
- **The one pro-permit voice** permitted a home addition specifically to protect resale: an unpermitted addition means an appraiser will not count the square footage at listing. He waited ~3 months for permits.
- **Tax disincentive (from a Realtor in the thread):** if you do permit, understate the project cost because next year's property-tax appraisal rises by the stated cost.
- **It may not need a permit:** a couple of commenters say a repair/rebuild of an existing deck likely needs none. Install fall-protection railing regardless.
- **If you do permit, it is not trivial:** "you'll need engineered drawings, not just something you put together. That and the plan review... Not just dropping in and getting one." Stacking material before permitting was called "out of order."
- **Weak, satellite-based enforcement** feeds the skip-it culture: advice to photograph everything (footers with a tape measure) and build fast because one suburban county buys updated satellite photos every 3 years to catch new structures.

**Why it is useful (and the tension it exposes):**

- Confirms homeowner CONFUSION is real (unsure if/when a permit applies; repair vs rebuild ambiguity; unaware of engineered-drawing plus plan-review requirements).
- But it cuts against a homeowner-outreach thesis: the default reaction to permit friction is to DODGE it, not to pay someone to do it right. Disincentives are concrete (tax bump, 3-month wait, engineering cost) and enforcement is loose. Willingness to PAY for help vs just skipping is the open question, and this thread leans toward skipping.
- Reinforces an earlier read (decision 2026-06-05) that clueless homeowners are reachable but may be low-value; the acute, pay-to-fix pain sits one notch up at owner-builders and small developers.
