# Architecture Firm CEO A (15-person Houston firm) - permit comment responses eat 2+ days per project

## Summary

Chance encounter at a national architecture convention. The CEO is a heavy AI user (thousands of AI chats in six months; she tracks her team's usage in analytics and her leadership team resists). About 20 minutes in she asked whether Interviewer 2 was the student doing a permitting study: she had received Interviewer 1's cold email and forwarded it to her in-house permit runner (Permit Runner A). Interviewer 2 then pivoted to explaining the product.

## Core pain

Every project gets permit comments. Her team, billed at ~$200/hr, spends 2+ days purely reformatting comment letters into structured responses before fixing anything. An AI that drafts and formats the response from uploaded comment letters would save real money immediately. Quote: "We used to try to get drawings through without any permit comments. That is not even possible anymore. It does not matter how accurate your drawings are. The people on the other end are just [incompetent]."

## Other points

- The "opinionated reviewer" problem is real. The local chapter of the professional association has a permit task force that escalated to the mayor's office, saw improvement, then watched it fall off again.
- The firm also works in the county (simpler, mainly fire codes on a sheet) and other jurisdictions.
- Permit Runner A manages permitting as a profit center: he runs the firm's permits AND permits for other firms/contractors.
- She uses UpCodes and noticed a drawing-review feature she has not explored.
- Planning to roll out one AI tool per week to her team to build adoption.

## Pitch and what she validated

The interviewers' product aggregates historical permit decisions from the county to build a defense structure when reviewers interpret codes differently from the rest of the county (a "personal legal team" angle). Features she validated: (1) auto-draft permit comment responses from uploaded comment letters, formatted properly; (2) flag which comments need real architectural fixes vs which can be argued; (3) a case database of county interpretations for pushing back.

## Signal

After the conversation Interviewer 2 told Interviewer 1: "That was definitely the most promising conversation in terms of a sale that I've had."
