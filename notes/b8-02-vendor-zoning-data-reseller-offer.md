# Vendor CEO A (Canadian zoning/permit data startup) - SaaS is dead, services win; reseller offer

## Context

The interviewers originally reached out because the vendor sat in the construction permitting space. By the time of the call, the interviewers had moved from permitting-transparency and code-compliance tools to an outbound lead-gen service. Interviewer 1 gave a short history: they built a tool that checked design plans against municipal codes, then learned the bigger pain was opinionated reviewers who interpret codes beyond what is written. They then built something that aggregated reviewer timelines per municipality so developers could know, for example, that a specific reviewer took nine months to permit previous three-story multifamily projects, letting developers time their loan draw and save six figures a month in interest. They then recognized that their AI operations layer was more valuable than the products built on it, and now source and contact leads for other companies and deliver demo-ready prospects.

## Vendor background (as stated)

- Civil engineer with a PhD in construction technology; ran a university research center for about six years focused on development approvals: international e-permitting systems, best practices, BIM/GIS integration, automated zoning and building code checking.
- Since 2020 runs a venture studio of built-environment startups; about six startups have come out of it.
- One studio startup digitized the building code and is moving into automatic compliance checking.
- His own current company holds parcel-level data (demographics, real estate, zoning, official plan, building permits, development approval applications) for about 80 municipalities. Mining historical development-application and permit data for trends and insights "has come up many times already." He said the interviewers were "onto something" in their earlier permitting insight idea.
- The company launched a conversational AI assistant equipped with many MCP connectors to give parcel-level information for lead generation.
- Long-time real estate agent (21 years): "everybody just wants leads... much easier to sell than anything else."
- Team: about 10 full time and 8 contractors. He is CEO and major shareholder, running it until he finds the right founders because it is the closest to his background.

## Key points and views

- "I'm very impressed by how fast you went through what took us about ten years."
- "The future is AI-enabled services... software is done. SaaS is dead, for the most part. It's all going to come down to services. The one service you can sell to anybody today is bringing them customers." The question is the strength of delivery.
- Venture-studio model: the studio comes in as a co-founder, a high-equity structure he acknowledges founders dislike ("I don't like it as a founder"), justified by lessons learned and hand-holding.
- Offer on the table: the interviewers run their own shop, the studio feeds them initial customers (its startups need US market entry), under a service contract, whether delivery is manual or AI. Specifically, a reseller relationship for the data product: commission on sales, ideally "replacing the salesperson, or at least helping the salesperson," with possible involvement in execution given the interviewers' domain knowledge.
- Interviewer 1's stated focus: mastering lead sourcing and outbound, then expanding to closing so they handle the whole pipeline. He said they would only work with products they believe in, noted another prospective service client in the same hour (potentially a five-figure deal), and could not commit on the call.

## Buy signal

Commission-based reseller structure; he will route them to the product manager for a demo after the co-founder conversation. Classified as pay-if-fixed / pay-on-results rather than a committed purchase.
