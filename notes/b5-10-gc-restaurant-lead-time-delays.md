# GC Ops Coordinator A (small Houston design-build restaurant GC) - lead times cascade through the sub schedule

## Summary
The coordinator runs ordering, accounting, paperwork and inspections for a tiny GC: owner, two project managers, and herself/himself. They build restaurants and small commercial (currently wrapping two restaurants, more in the pipeline) and are trying to shift from residential toward commercial. Two big jobs run about 7-12 months plus several smaller ones.

## Workflow
- Project managers on site text, call, or write into Smartsheet (what to order, who to call). The coordinator acts on that. Mornings bring texts from subs (need paperwork or materials) and PMs (schedule the fire-line company for final inspection, find a hinge with specific measurements).
- Paperwork = inspection certifications (sprinkler, alarm, hood) routed to the fire marshal, plus site instructions (e.g. a sign telling subs to close windows on new builds so rain does not ruin floors). Missing or unapproved paperwork falls on the coordinator and delays the job.
- Ordering: match the exact spec, check lead time; pick up if needed now, ship if it can wait. Inventory is a manual list; most items are in stock or from a big-box store; special orders are the risk.
- About 6 subs per job (electrical, plumbing, HVAC); use the owner's preferred sub if any, else their own.

## Pains
- **Lead times and cascade.** Subs are sequenced: sub two waits on sub one, sub one waits on a part. A special-order 5/16 bronze bar with a lead time into early June threatened the owner's deadline, forcing a plan B (paint a substitute). The PM knew but did not flag it in time because he was stretched across two finishing projects with nitpicky owners: human overload, not a tool failure (PMs are active in Smartsheet).
- **Sub reliability.** Subs say they will start early and show at 1pm. Accountability means being "the mean person"; telling them their check depends on finishing motivates them. Unreliable ones are fired.
- **Inspectors.** City and fire inspectors work on their own schedule and give no leeway, adding delay.

## Estimating and sales
- Bottom-up: collect bids from usual electrician, plumber, HVAC; add materials, labor, a design fee if they designed it, plus profit. Owners typically bid ~4 GCs and choose on price and trust. Payment ~30% up front, invoice at completion, owner holds a retainage paid at the end.
- Marketing: word of mouth, repeat restaurant clients (one wants them for another location and a sushi concept), van wraps, flyers, door hangers, social.

## Tools
Smartsheet for schedule, tasks, photos, documents, comments and checkboxes ("if it's not broke don't fix it"). Heard of Procore and used it once on a landscape/park job but never got the hang of it.

## Signal
Strong material-optimization and lead-time signal, but a pure project-management tool is a hard sell because Smartsheet is adopted and good enough. The wedge is material timing and lead-time risk, not another tracker. Overlaps with schedule slippage, estimating accuracy and sub assessment from the GC side.
