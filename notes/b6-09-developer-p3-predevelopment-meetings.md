# Developer B (P3 land developer, mid-size Texas city) - pre-development meetings and a one-stop shop

## Setup
Developer B's firm is a public-private partnership developer with ~1,000 acres where the city owns the land and the firm is the private partner. This gives deep, ongoing relationships with city staff and lets them write their own planned development with flexible uses. P3 does not mean special treatment or speed; it means efficiency because staff are partners, are briefed early, and there are no hidden agendas. They still go through the same formal process. First call is always the civil engineer.

## Process as they run it
1. Concept/site plan: civil engineer builds a CAD file from base-map data (no survey yet). Take it to the city's development services for a pre-development meeting with planners, engineers, fire and utilities in one room, about 30 minutes of quick feedback.
2. Preliminary plan: more detail (dimensions, utility corridors, neighbors, use designations); surveyor sets metes and bounds if parcels are carved out.
3. Construction drawings.
4. Plat to finalize the land division.
Commercial buildings: architect designs and submits to the city's commercial building unit.

## The ping-pong
Submissions circulate to all departments, each with a comment window (~2 weeks per cycle here). Best case 2 rounds; with no prep 4-6. Typical comments: wrong phasing, wrong survey reference, missing check valve, wrong code year; mostly easy to fix once the pattern is known. Pre-development meetings cut rounds by surfacing big issues before the formal clock starts.

## City comparison
His city: one-stop development services (water, sewer, storm, variances, fire, rezoning, planning commission, council) in one building; ~80% of questions answered there. Houston: three separate offices (county, city planning/development, the permitting center) pointing to one another, and no zoning, which changes the process.

## Sample review-committee notices (from the shared documents)
- Preliminary plan for a ~71-lot, ~27-acre subdivision phase: decision "Revisions Required." Comments from five departments (committee chair, planning, engineering, water, plus transportation/electric on the plat): plan cannot be approved until the associated rezoning clears council; trail fall-away zones; fire hydrant location must match construction plans; show a 25-ft sewer easement.
- Final plat for a 12-lot, ~13-acre phase: also "Revisions Required": cannot go to the planning commission until the preliminary plan clears; filing location of the sewer easement (a repeat comment); mark streets as private with HOA maintenance and provide an access easement; add a 15-ft electric easement. Construction documents (drainage, estimate, sewer, water reports) already accepted.
- Reading: two rounds on adjacent phases, five departments per round; comments range from minor (update a note) to structural (show an easement); repeat comments appear when a prior fix was missed. A mid-prep cycle.
