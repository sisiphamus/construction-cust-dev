# Concrete Sub Owner A (30-year New York concrete contractor) - no marketing, owner-direct relationships, supplier quality slipping

Referral-based conversation. Interviewer's impression: excellent customer service; "sales isn't getting replaced."

## Business
- "We specialize in concrete. Everything we do is concrete. A jack of all trades and a master of none."
- Started in concrete about 30 years ago, bought a van roughly five years in, and has run his own firm since. Early on he met people who never paid him and made many mistakes ("learned the hard way").
- Crew of 20-25, with a long-tenured team leader (25 years) and four other team leaders. Jobs run $5K to $500K. He meets the customer, looks at the job, prices it and signs it off. Everyone has his cell number. A 19-year-old does the paperwork.
- Work hours 7:00am to 2:30pm. Busy season May to October; December to March concrete cannot be poured properly in New York (cure problems), so winter work is demolition and excavation.
- Top priority: avoid accidents so everyone goes home. Each job is done as quickly and efficiently as possible.

## Labor
- Crews are hard-labor, mostly non-college, and heavily family-connected; he hires by referral from existing crew ("I need another guy"). He pays top money and accommodates days off and paperwork help; in return they work hard. His first employee has been with him 29 years. Past hires from another source could not do the work and were cut off; he tries not to fire people or be nasty.

## Customers, marketing and sales
- Zero marketing today. After 30 years: ~25-30% referrals, ~80% from people seeing his work and asking for his number (percentages as stated). Some customers have used him for everything for 30 years. Early on he used business cards and community-newspaper advertising; "one job and another, like a tree".
- He likes customers calling him directly; customers like dealing with the owner ("with a big company you never get to the owner").
- If a mistake happens they rip it out and redo it at no charge; "they never disappear".
- Payment risk: some customers bullied him out of payment early on. Now, if he does not get the right vibe he will not give an estimate; he describes himself as a great judge of character.

## Technology view
Embraces email, video calls and phones, but "AI can never take the work I do." He does not expect robots to change his work: "The world will never be changed in construction... for the most part." Not planning to retire.

## Pain
Over the past 2 years the ready-mix concrete company he buys from has started skimping on quality and delivering worse concrete.

## Quotes
- "Always do the right thing... you'll be amazed how it will come back to you. It doesn't cost anything to be kind."
- "I love my work. I love my employees. I love my customers."
