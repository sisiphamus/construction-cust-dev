# Painting Contractor A (small commercial/residential painter) - low-bid pressure and hiring

(Contact asked not to be named.)

- Typical day: meet the crew at the job, make sure materials are there and everyone shows up and works, pick up lunch and more materials as needed. 2 painters on staff; used to be bigger. "Good work leads to more work. If you cheat somebody, the internet is unforgiving."
- Main pain: low-ball bidding. He might bid a house at $5k and a competitor goes behind his back and offers $1k using workers paid very little per day. He says he is not cheapest but offers peace of mind and trust.
- Vetting a new painter: ask what the toughest sheen to paint correctly is (satin); watch how they open a can of paint; terminology ("pale" vs "pot") marks an old-school painter; check how well they clean brushes after. Pays $25/hour trial and watches the first hour "like a hawk," checking back often. Solid painters earn $35-40/hour. Sees day-labor pickups as a gamble. Background checks are needed because some hires have histories he does not want on a customer's property; one skilled but troubled hire did not work out.
- Materials: sourcing is an issue; typically buys the day before.
- A customer once accused his crew of taking something during an interior job.
