# Sub B (small pest control company co-owner) - owner-operator admin overload

## Takeaways
- Day to day is nonstop; he is nearing a breaking point. Between him and his partner they cover HR, sales, QA, compliance and fleet maintenance. At 7-8 vehicles "you are pretty much not a little guy anymore" and "everything gets easier because of revenue" (they can hire quality help). They expect to cross that line by year-end.
- Averages 50-70 phone calls a day plus emails from job sites during peak season (April to October, when bugs come out); roughly half that in the off season. Contracts run year-round.
- He prefers to handle things as they come in rather than call back; when he stops answering the customer complaint is immediate, and a common complaint about companies that get big is "I could never reach them anymore."
- Quality of work is the number one reputation driver; scheduling slips are tolerable. He has friends across the service trades (GCs, painters).
- Fragmented tools / admin tax: no sign he has software to absorb the call and admin load (a lead-gen/admin-automation opening).

## Hiring
- One tech per vehicle, 6 full-time now, recruited via job boards.
- Resumes do not help; he screens on answers to questions like what they do in their free time. Family-oriented people with longevity at a previous employer are best. He hires people brand new to the trade, not experienced ones, because they say "I used to do it this way."
- Young people are not inclined toward jobs where you break a sweat.

## Market and views
- Gets 3-4 acquisition emails a week (private equity or similar) and has seen it grow every year since COVID.
- Expects his industry to be impacted positively; thinks it is 10-20 years before AI takes his kind of jobs. Believes the tech/IT golden age is ending.
- Pest patterns: warehouses get rodents from outside; food distribution warehouses get roaches arriving in imported produce packaging that then spread to supermarkets and restaurants. Food businesses are mandated to have pest control.
