# Pest Control Owner A (small pest control company) - owner-operator admin overload

## Key points
- Day to day is nonstop; he is "reaching a breaking point". At 7-8 vehicles "you are not a little guy anymore" and everything gets easier because of revenue; they expect to cross that by year-end. Until then he and his partner cover HR, sales, QA, compliance and fleet maintenance, and plan to hire quality help afterward.
- "From the moment I wake up I'm receiving phone calls from different job sites, emails": 50-70 calls a day in peak season (April to October, when bugs come out); about half in the off season, though contracts run year-round. He answers as things come in rather than calling back, and when he does not he sees the negative effect immediately. A common customer complaint about growing firms: "once the company got big I could never reach them anymore."
- Quality of work is number one across service trades; scheduling can slip but quality drives reputation.
- Staffing: one tech per vehicle, 6 full-time techs, hired via online job boards. Resumes do not help; he screens on answers to questions like what they do in their free time. He prefers family-oriented candidates and longevity at prior employers, and hires brand-new-to-the-trade people because experienced hires say "I used to do it this way". Young people are less inclined to do sweaty work.
- Gets 3-4 buyout emails a week from venture or private-equity-backed consolidators, increasing yearly since COVID.
- Outlook: his industry will be impacted positively in coming years; "10-20 years till AI really starts taking our jobs"; he thinks the golden age of tech jobs is ending.
- Demand drivers: warehouses get rodents from outside; food distribution warehouses get roaches arriving in imported packages, passed on to supermarkets and restaurants. Food businesses are mandated to have pest control.
