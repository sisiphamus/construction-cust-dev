# Developer B (fully integrated developer) - order materials ahead of groundbreaking, pick subs on track record

## Process

- Picks where to build using market saturation, rent rates, cost and similar. Fully integrated: acquisition, development team, construction management, finance.
- Works with GCs "all over," bids out every time to a pool of GCs. Most people send to ~4 GCs and compare estimates. His advice: instead, look inside each GC's estimate: why each sub was picked, whether each is cheap or expensive. Example: a GC had a very cheap concrete sub that was a small unknown firm; the sub turned out to be owned by a large firm, and the five prior buildings with that GC built trust.
- As an attorney by background he works with zoning attorneys and knows roughly when he will get permits and works around that. Everyone has an expected groundbreaking date; before it you buy land, bid out GCs and start buying materials early, because investors want returns and workers must be paid.
- Typical sequence: start early, get bids in, contractors go to subs, collect numbers, pick the GC and lock it up with a contract.

## Materials pain and tactics

- Long lead times on materials are "totally true." Order before the date you can break ground.
- Metal buildings: some metal subs buy coil by the hundreds months in advance, store it and sell/deliver as prices rise. Find subs storing coil and beams; they have better pricing.
- Prices spike on trade-policy anticipation; buying domestic steel is more reliable, while importers are hurt by tariffs.
- Recent job: shortage of thick beams; a structural engineer welded two thinner beams together. Wider spans need thicker beams.
- Roofing and electrical components from China; some elevator parts only come from China and elevators could not be built during the pandemic shortage. Now they know the risk and plan for it.
- Mostly builds block, tilt wall or insulated metal panel, not wood frame (wood-frame colleagues have lumber price swings). "It's all about supply."
- Labor got very expensive; less construction work recently. Higher interest rates mean projects pencil out less. Site shutdowns can happen when a sub's crew has labor-compliance problems even if the developer did nothing wrong.

## Software and sub selection

- Uses Procore: "all GCs and architects are on it." Keeps RFIs, drawings and pictures. Some GCs refuse it and he hesitates to hire them. The construction manager uses it more than he does. Not used for bidding; bids are leveled internally.
- Sub selection is experience only: a list of known subs; the cheapest sub is often always late, so not hired; a sub who did something wrong on the last job is not hired.
- Quality-control example: a soil supplier delivered contaminated soil; testing found it; the supplier made the hauler return with good soil. Lesson: the construction manager should inspect soil on arrival and check the ticket for origin.
- "Everything I'm telling you is from issues I have run into."
