# GC Operations Coordinator A (small Houston design-build GC, restaurants) - lead times cascade through sequenced subs

## Summary

Small Houston design-build GC: owner, two project managers, and this person who runs ordering, accounting, paperwork and inspections. They build restaurants and small commercial (finishing two restaurants plus new builds and sushi concepts, in Houston and The Woodlands). The dominant pain is material lead times and special-order delays that cascade through a tightly sequenced sub schedule. Sub coordination is second: ~6 subs per job, unreliable ones get dropped, payment timing motivates slackers. Customer-dev interview, in person.

## Signal

- Strong material-timing signal: lead-time and special-order risk is the explicit, repeated pain and directly delays jobs.
- Schedule slippage, cost estimating and sub assessment are all present from the GC side.
- Smartsheet is adopted and "if it's not broke don't fix it," so a pure project-management tool is a hard sell; the wedge would be material timing / lead time, not another tracker.

## Details

- **Daily flow:** project managers text, call or write into a shared smartsheet with requests ("we need to order this, call this place"); the coordinator orders from that. Subs also text each morning (need paperwork or materials), as do PMs (schedule the fire-line company for final inspections; find a door hinge with specific measurements).
- **Paperwork:** inspection certifications (sprinkler, alarm, hood) route through the coordinator to the fire marshal, plus resource tasks. Missing or unapproved paperwork falls on her and delays the job.
- **Ordering:** match the measurement (e.g., a 4-inch bronze hinge), then check lead times. If needed now, pick it up; if it can wait a week, ship. Delays cascade because subs are sequenced: sub two waits on sub one waits on a part.
- **Example:** a special-order 5/16 bronze bar has a lead time reaching about June 5-6; the owner wants the job finished before that, so plan B is a spray-painted substitute. The PM knew but did not flag it in time because he was stretched across two finishing projects with nit-picky owners (human overload, not a tool failure; PMs are active in the tracker).
- **Inventory:** manual list; most needs are in stock or bought at big-box stores; special orders are the risk.
- **Subs:** about 6 per job (electrical, plumbing, HVAC and others). If the restaurant owner has a preferred sub they use them; otherwise their own. Subs slack ("start early morning, show at 1 pm"); accountability is hard, "you have to be the mean person." Telling them their check depends on finishing motivates them.
- **Inspectors** (city, fire marshal) work on their own schedule, give no leeway, and add delay.
- **Tools:** everything runs on Smartsheet (attach images, documents, comments, checkboxes, screenshots of placed orders with delivery dates). Tried Procore once on another firm's park job and did not get the hang of it.
- **Marketing:** word of mouth and repeat restaurant clients (one wants them for a second location and a sushi concept), van wraps, flyers, door hangers, social. Trying to move from residential toward commercial. Two big jobs running 7-12 months plus several smaller ones.
- **Estimating:** collect bids from their usual electrician, plumber and HVAC subs, add materials, labor, a design fee if they designed it, plus profit. Owners bid out to ~4 GCs and choose on price and trust.
- **Payment:** ~30% up front, invoice the whole project at completion, owner holds a retainer paid at the end.
