# Engineer A (federal-complex site equipment engineer) - vendor trust and fragmented paper systems

Informal conversation, loosely related to construction (equipment procurement and maintenance rather than building).

## Role

Develops the equipment and maintenance side of a new special project: spends time with vendors, reads manuals, writes maintenance procedures and sends them to a technical writer, builds spare-parts lists and organizes everything. An engineer is assigned to each piece of equipment and writes the acceptance test and requirements; he does the test himself to learn the equipment. Mechanics will do more of the hands-on work as it scales and he will stay for troubleshooting. He expects the site to buy more equipment in future.

## Pains

- **Acceptance tests fail.** He spent about three weeks at a factory in another state running an acceptance test that failed, then had to go back, develop a scope and bring the vendor to the site to learn directly from them.
- **Vendors without maintenance routines.** They often buy specialized equipment from vendors that have no maintenance routines, so the site writes them. One vendor who makes great machines was overwhelmed with work and could not even support a test during a week-long visit.
- **Vendor selection and trust.** At his previous lab he saw how equipment is chosen: one American vendor met all specs and wanted the site's business ("for a lot of vendors the site is just another number"). On the government side trust is very important. Once a vendor works well you stick with them, like a reliable car brand; it also makes buying easier.
- **Fragmented, paper-heavy tools.** Vendors use Word documents, the site uses maintenance-management/asset databases, communication is by email then put into reports, and there are document repositories. Hands-on work is moving from paper to mobile, but "it's a big task to change." Inefficient.
- **Culture:** the research lab relies on people's skills with procedures; the production site is top-down, "old manufacturing mindset," wants people to rely on checklists.
