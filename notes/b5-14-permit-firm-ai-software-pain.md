# Permit Firm Staff A and Founder A (2-person Washington permit firm) - permitting software that does not check code

## Business
Two-person firm serving builders: run plan sets to reviewers, coordinate civil and geotechnical engineers and other specialists, handle approvals and procedures for zoning, land use and architectural code.

## Jurisdiction variation
- Counties in the region typically adopt the same base codes, but amendments, customizations and interpretation vary by jurisdiction.
- Interpretation flexibility varies by discipline: architects have little room; civil and geo engineers have more room to negotiate alternatives.
- Drainage is the single most common reason plans are rejected in Washington, and a blind spot for current software.

## Relationships and pre-development meetings
- Relationships with permit departments matter, but turnover is high (staff move roles and jurisdictions), so new staff do not know historical decisions and relationships must be rebuilt.
- Pre-development meetings vary: some jurisdictions help design the plan; others "just say no" and throw rules without helping toward approval. Best practice: know what each jurisdiction wants to see, arrive with civil engineer plans, and coordinate civil/geo/MEP early.

## Software pain
The county permit portal software (ProjectDOX, similar to Avolve) only does administrative work (page orientation, numbering, technical correctness). It does not check code compliance or interpret building, zoning or land-use codes, and is hard to use. Result: busy work that makes permitting harder; users still do the real compliance work manually.

## Relevance
Opening for permitting intelligence and code-checking automation, jurisdiction-specific requirement mapping, and plan review automation as a replacement for admin-only portals. No stated purchase intent.
