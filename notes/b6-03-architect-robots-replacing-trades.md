# Architect A (principal at a large A/E firm) - robots are replacing trades faster than expected

## Context
Interviewer 2 approached Architect A after a conference session on reimagining malls. The first part of the recording is the session Q&A: a city planner described using an administrative-review checklist (meet the intent, several ways to do it) so redevelopment of a dead mall does not need to go back to the planning commission, and an audience member argued for extending the street grid. The rest is the 1:1.

## What she said
- She designed malls and shopping centers, concluded around 2018 that the retail model was dying, retrained, and now does industrial and advanced-manufacturing architecture.
- Proof point from her own work: the same building type and square footage in distribution centers, fulfillment centers and plants needed ~1,200 parking stalls per shift six years ago and ~700 now, because roughly half those jobs were eliminated by automation.
- Her claim: robots will replace construction-adjacent labor, including electrical work, sooner than people expect. Data centers are being built in ~18 months with temporary labor, then staffed by robots. Limiting factor is human adoption speed, not technology (analogy: robot vacuums). She cited humanoid home robots in the high-teens thousand dollars range coming to market within a year.
- Advice: anyone designing buildings (residential, hospital, school) should account for this trajectory now or "have errors later." Healthcare facility upgrades driven by seismic code deadlines (around 2030 in California) will then flatten to population growth.
- Drones: delivery is the simplest use case; hospital blood/organ delivery as an idea.

## Interviewer's pitch and her reaction
Interviewer 2 described a project on permit approvals and code interpretation: software already scans plans against code, but the gap is when a reviewer insists on an arbitrary interpretation. The concept is to act like a "legal team" by citing how other reviewers in the same office have interpreted the code. He also floated housing for data-center workers as a bottleneck area. She engaged but gave no buy signal or comment on the permit problem.

## Counterpoint from the interviewer
He doubted a 3-year horizon for construction robots because of confined environments; a layout-marking robot startup founder had told him they chose that stage because it is the most open space. Trades like electricians and plumbers involve more problem solving than assembly-line tasks. She agreed on the difficulty but held the timeline.

## Takeaways
- Useful as a long-horizon labor-automation view from a senior architect; no pain data on permitting or code interpretation.
- Data center boom cited as validation that construction is a growth sector.
- Buy signal: none.
