# SaaS Vendor Rep A (California code and permit workflow startup) - selling to jurisdictions and architects

Conversation at an architecture conference booth, started by Interviewer 2 (introducing himself as a student founder).

## Product and approach
- Code compliance and permit workflow SaaS: 30+ California jurisdictions (including Sacramento, Long Beach and Bay Area cities), ~10-person team. Works with architects (who spend ~70% of their time on admin and communication), jurisdictions and GCs.
- Current focus is "workflows": zone analysis, document completeness check, QC workflows.
- Code database: licensed ICC codes, books split by year and code group, RAG search. Municipal amendments are tracked partly through automation and partly by customer request (not a team calling every city hall); also scraping and manual work.
- Does not integrate with legacy permit systems (such as electronic plan review platforms); runs as a fully web-based side tool, betting the legacy tools will die.

## Go-to-market insights
- Selling to jurisdictions is "not as hard as you think" if you find people in real pain: they are severely understaffed and it takes ~5 years to train a plan reviewer.
- Entry tactic: get in via a credit-card-limit budget (avoids full procurement approval), then upsell annually.
- Org insight: plan checkers do not buy; the senior building official does. Jurisdictions are friendly but you must understand their org structure before selling.
- Trust challenge: when pressed on code accuracy, he acknowledged AI outputs must be verifiable and said he hears this problem repeatedly.
