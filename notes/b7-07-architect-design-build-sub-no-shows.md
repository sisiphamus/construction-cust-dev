# Design-Build Architect A (Houston design-build firm) - subs not showing up, material volatility, heavy AI use

## Summary

Design-Build Architect A runs a Houston design-build firm and has been in architecture since the late 1990s. The call had poor audio (dropouts), so parts of the transcript are fragmentary.

## Key points

**Subs not showing up (biggest pain)**
- Subs, who are typically their own business owners, commit to a Tuesday morning; the firm rearranges the whole schedule around them; they then say "something came up, I'll be out Wednesday." He sees this as miscommunication or not understanding that full schedules are built around their appointed time.
- He considers it "not necessarily a software solution; it's managing a person," managing expectations and consequences. The firm has let subs go over it even when the work quality was good: "I'm doing good work, it doesn't matter, you're not doing the work at the appointed time."
- Causes: overbooked, mismanaged calendars, or a prior job running long without warning anyone. Subs do not think about how their delay affects others.
- Workarounds: tell everyone to arrive at the same time as the key sub, and send automatic reminders the day of.

**Seasonality**
- Slower around the holidays; clients sometimes decide late, pushing work further out. The firm uses an off-the-shelf time-management tool that works well.

**Estimating and bids**
- Estimates use prior project metrics (what it actually came in at, overages), plus bid requests to the various trades.
- Out-of-control factors: imported items, tariffs and erratic market prices; hurricane-season rain-outs (lost three to four weeks in a past season) followed by material cost increases and shortages. Labor is locked in with employees.
- Materials come through wholesale accounts with discounts; this helps control prices only a little.

**Differentiation**
- As design-build, they already have the client relationship as architect, so the transition into construction is natural and they usually do not go out to competitive bid after design; competition happens on the design front end with other architects.
- Lead sources: (1) referral, (2) online presence (mostly Google), (3) AI: people ask an AI assistant and it recommends the firm.

**AI usage**
- "We absolutely use AI in every single capacity" except actual drawing: code research, pre-development, proposals, infographics, quick renderings (a competitive edge when showing clients what the firm can provide), and a little invoicing. Tip: take an image you like and ask AI to break it down into a prompt.
