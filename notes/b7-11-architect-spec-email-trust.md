# Architect A (250-person firm) - spec management and email automation trust

## Summary

An architect at a national architecture conference, from a ~250-person firm, had an extended conversation with Interviewer 1 and Interviewer 2 about the difficulty of building AI tools for architecture workflows. He was frustrated and empathetic: "it took me months to understand what specifications were," so he understands why it is hard.

**Specs.** The firm keeps a large Excel master database of spec sections covering everything from wall construction to stone type. For each project they pull relevant sections (for example, "we want carpet in part of this project," so pull the carpet spec), insert each manufacturer's installation requirements, and weave them together section by section. A medium project's spec book runs about 1,000 pages; a large building like the conference venue would be about 10,000 pages. A tool must work for a 12-person firm doing it in Word, a 250-person firm with a cataloged library, and a 2,500-person firm with 100+ years of archived documents.

**Email trust.** About 100 emails per day per project. Knowing which are follow-ups, reference-only, or immediate and hot is where automation would matter. They tried an Outlook plug-in to surface the "10 hottest outstanding items" for a school project. It missed a critical one: a manufacturer discontinuing the door type they had ordered, with doors due on site the following week. Missing that email costs a lot of money and killed trust in the tool. "Trust is one of those things that could be slow to earn and fast to lose." Older, long-practicing architects abandon a tool the first time it fails and go back to doing it themselves.

## Key quotes

- On workflow diversity: "You have to be dynamic in how you can apply your tools because it has to work for a 12-person firm and my 250 and somebody else's 2,500-person firm... You don't do residential projects the same way you do a hospital." Interviewer 2 added that a public school has its own guidelines, vendor relationships and standards, unlike a private school.
- On targeting: "This is where it gets hard with regards to not being actual architects... this is a whole career worth of things I have to explain."
- A CEO elsewhere had wished for a tool that digs up all past emails and carries context from previous projects into the next application (raised by Interviewer 1).

None.

## Actions

- Architect offered to check out an email-triage plug-in and report back.
