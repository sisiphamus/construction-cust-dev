# GC VP A (Houston retail/commercial GC) - delays, sub management, tech adoption

## Role
Responsible cradle to grave: business development (calling developers, discussing the market and future projects), conceptual pricing, helping manage costs in preconstruction, guiding owners with their architect or engineer, sometimes working with the city, developing contract scope and language with legal, project start-up (logs, mobilization), schedule accuracy, handing the estimate to the field, facilitating subcontractor buyout, and managing the warranty period. "I manage the people that manage the work." Project managers handle buyout of materials.

## Schedules
Schedules are a living document that change with change orders; delays on schedule are not frequent. Rough split: ~75% on time, ~25% a bit over, and in ~85% of those a documented reason such as a design change. Hurricanes can mean 6 months or a million dollars of damage. Owners want projects faster (interest on loans), and the GC pays a premium for team members who can use advanced software. Projects are built faster than before, though some schedule stretches (12 months became 14-15) are attributed to technology and complexity.

## Causes of delays (consistent)
- Permitting: went from 3-4 weeks to 3-4 months, with fewer people reviewing plans; apartment/residential development has exploded.
- Weather.
- Materials: a supplier was meant to deliver doors last week and still has no date, forcing temporary doors (cost and time, then removal).
- Labor: many Spanish-speaking crews; with immigration enforcement in town work slows because workers get worried.
- A fuel price spike tied to an overseas conflict raises diesel costs; everything moves by truck, driving up concrete and steel. Suppliers occasionally email that prices will rise ~15%. Steel is bought from one supplier given all the info, with little back and forth unless calling something out.

## Subs
- Always issues with subs, usually over money: the GC is not a bank and pays subs only after the owner pays the GC.
- Selection: safety record and a prequalification package (documenting they pay bills, vendors and employees, no lawsuits, no accidents or fatalities). Based on who you know; never rely on one trade partner; rule of thumb is 3 bids so outliers can be explained.
- Loyalty example: an HVAC sub from 20 years ago did only one grocery client's work and put that client first over everyone.

## Winning work
Chase developers, introduce the company, get on bidder lists; retail bids typically against 3-4 contractors and "99% of the time they go with the lowest bidder in retail." Word of mouth matters. A first job with a regional grocery chain came in under budget and two weeks early, which won more work; lunches, pricing, being seen and relevant. About 85% of retail work comes from the owner's designer, then bid; sometimes the GC partners on design.

## Documentation
RFIs sent when problems appear; cost of changes tracked; swap requests. Meetings and photos are legal documents (to prove it actually rained, or that a directive was given); meeting minutes are sent so they stand.

## Tools
Procore (versatile: document control, photo documentation, accounting, meeting agendas/minutes, coordination), Phoenix scheduling, Microsoft Office/Excel for tracking, a CRM (Salesforce) for business development, and Smartsheet at some owners' request; school districts use their own systems. Older tools (Prolog-style project management, expedition tools) became outdated and not versatile. Shifting everything toward Procore. Beginning to explore AI estimating software ("takes time and money to determine whether" it works). Market is moving toward AI and equipment; many construction people are "old school... lots of technology and not a lot of users because they are old people who won't change." Standardizes project management format across projects.

## Buy signal / next steps
Curious about tech for estimating, document control, project management, accounting and scheduling. Gave referrals to several other GC contacts for further interviews (follow up).

## Interview prep questions the interviewers used
Day-in-the-life, business development to contract, sub sourcing/vetting, owner relations, biggest causes of delays, procurement decisions, tools, how schedules stay correct.
