# Architect B (CEO, 15-person Houston architecture firm) - permit comment responses eat 2+ days

## Summary

Chance encounter at a national architecture convention. Architect B is a heavy AI user and CEO of a 15-person Houston firm (commercial interiors, medical, corporate). The first ~40 minutes were AI adoption (her leadership team resists; she tracks individual usage in analytics; plans to roll out one AI tool per week to her team; CEO roundtable groups all wrestling with AI; she is a skeptic of AI scaremongering). Around minute 40 she asked if Interviewer 1 was from Rice doing a permitting study: she had received Interviewer 2's cold email and forwarded it to Permit Runner A, the firm's in-house permit runner. Interviewer 1 then explained the product.

## Core pain

Every project gets permit comments. Her team (billing around $200/hr) spends 2+ days purely reformatting comment letters into structured responses before fixing anything. "We used to try to get drawings through without any permit comments. That is not even possible anymore. It does not matter how accurate your drawings are." An AI that drafts and formats the response from uploaded comment letters would save real money immediately.

She also confirmed the opinionated-reviewer problem: the local architects' association has a permit task force that escalated to the mayor's office, got improvement, then watched it fall off again.

## Direction she validated

1. Auto-draft permit comment responses from uploaded comment letters, formatted properly.
2. Flag which comments need real architectural fixes vs which can be argued.
3. A case database of county/city interpretations to support pushback when a reviewer interprets codes differently from the rest of the county (the interviewers' pitch: aggregate historical permit decisions to build a defense structure; "personal legal team" framing). She was receptive.

## Other details

- The firm also works in the county (simpler, mainly fire code sheets, which she called repeatable, tedious tasks) and other jurisdictions.
- Permit Runner A manages permitting as a profit center: runs the firm's permits and also does permitting for other firms and contractors.
- She uses an existing code-reference tool and noticed a "drawing review" feature she had not explored.
- Interviewer 1 to Interviewer 2 afterwards: "the most promising conversation in terms of a sale that I've had."
