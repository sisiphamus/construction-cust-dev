# Freight Forwarder A (Middle East ocean freight forwarder) - AI to automate documentation and cut labor cost

## Business
- ~70 people (about 15 sales), departments for operations, customs brokerage and accounting, branches in 3 places. ~95% importing ocean containers (mostly China), ~3% air, ~2% export. Strong in other regions as well. 20-25K containers a year.
- Differentiators: deep knowledge of the China market, the best China partner agents, strong volume so better rates than small forwarders and good carrier contracts. Opening an order from China is "plug and play": a customer gets an update within 10 minutes. Origin-side cost depends on partner agents; a good US agent must know shipment handling and rates.

## What holds up a shipment
- Far-East port congestion: no space, everything booked ~2 weeks ahead.
- Customs release: customers must give approvals/documents to authorities and sometimes forget.
- Last-mile trucking: after release, truckers are scarce (many tied up by regional conflict logistics), fuel costs are up, and truckers slow to confirm or cancel at the last minute for better offers; delays of 4-5 days, then the forwarder must apologize to the customer.

## Main issue: employee costs
- Up 50-60% since COVID; pays in local currency but earns in dollars. Same worldwide, with US forwarders outsourcing to offshore call centers; only the US customs broker is domestic.
- Young people avoid logistics; people stay 1-2 years then leave; finding good employees is very hard.
- Response: push AI to replace menial work. Documentation entry is "90% AI now"; a pilot loads customs shipments with AI (commercial invoices were manual; now uploaded, read by AI, written to the ERP, and a person checks). Automatic customer notifications when a vessel ETA changes, tied to the ERP. Hiring profile shifted from fast typists to people who can work with AI. He wants to keep the same staff and make them more productive to lower cost-to-revenue, not cut headcount. An internal person leads AI/tech.
- ERP: all accounting, customs clearance and transmission run in one ERP used daily; only 3-4 local ERPs exist because big global ERPs avoid the market (customs integration, local hosting), creating a quasi-monopoly; the product is basically unchanged from 20 years ago, though AI tools are being added. A staffer continually pushes the vendor for features.
- Personal note on AI: he avoids using AI personally because he does not want one system holding all his data, but sees the cost and revenue upside for the business.

## Read
Useful analogy for document-heavy, labor-cost-driven AI automation. Not a construction buyer.
