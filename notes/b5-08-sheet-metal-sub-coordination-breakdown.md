# Sheet Metal Sub A (Midwest industrial fabricator) - coordination across trades is the pain

## Context
Industrial sheet metal (duct work, soybean and food-processing plants), 4 shops in two Midwest locations, ~200 people. Currently building factories for a huge agribusiness client across the US and now Europe. He describes himself as a "make it happen" guy, not a seller. "Construction is broken."

## Pains
- **Materials.** Mills control the flow of steel and he is at their mercy. Mills ship coil that is hard to process, so he buys through distributors who cut it to usable form (processing needs special equipment). Distributors struggle to get enough. Waiting weeks for materials; a recent plant needed ~50 workers' output. He has hired someone just to drive around and get a foot in the door with mills. Scaling means buying lasers and plasma cutters (one laser ~$1M "and that's cheap").
- **Labor.** Few people want the trades; in the Midwest it is a hard sell, and people have been retiring for 5-6 years without replacement. Data centers being built everywhere are absorbing hundreds of workers at a time. Bonus handed out early on a current job and then people stopped showing up; new crew arriving for the last month.
- **Communication and coordination (biggest).** Four shops to keep aligned; "even with all the tech, something always gets left out or built twice." One engineering error can scrap ~$15K of custom-fabricated material, and sheet metal is the last trade that makes its own parts from scratch.
- **Competing CAD plans.** Every trade (roofer, plumber, sheet metal) does its own CAD; they communicate but are not on the same page, so a job starts as a race to install before the other guy's plan collides with yours. His firm GPS-lays out and 3D-scans buildings but nobody merges all trades' layouts into one cohesive layout. Collision reports exist on some jobs but are inconsistent and only list clashes; the trades fix them. Even when an engineering firm designs everything and subs out work, different plans circulate.
- **Rotating GCs.** Too many hands in the pot; the GC is supposed to direct trades but rarely does. Better GCs come from a trade. GCs get swapped out mid-job when things go wrong, so no one sees a job through. Wants the general present from start to finish. Also thinks engineers who design should have to build it once.
- **Tools.** Teams and email ("WTF is happening"). He writes things down and copies ~15 people on shipping emails. Large jobs use a shared project portal (he thinks it is ACC) where someone must manually update everyone's status; the owner's team employs a person per trade to update it, partly to have documentation if a dispute ends in court. "Hundreds of people in the background pushing paper." Thousands of people touch big industrial jobs; hard to know who is working where (e.g. can electricians get in with 10 lifts in the space).

## Ideas voiced
- A better way to keep everyone in the loop; a way to sort and overlay trades' plans (even projecting overlapping 2D plans and turning them into 3D).
- Data centers take ~3 years to build and GPUs are outdated by then; speed is money, so faster data-center building "could print billions".
- Advice: "Go to an area, find a problem, solve it."

## Read
Real, repeated pain, but not an obvious buyer: this is an anecdotal wish for multi-trade plan overlay and a single source of truth. No stated purchase intent.
