# Painting Sub Owner A (small commercial/residential painting contractor) - low-bid pressure, hiring and vetting, material sourcing

The owner asked that his name not be recorded; he was friendly and talkative.

## Typical day and business

- Meets the crew at the job, makes sure materials are there and everyone shows up and works, takes lunch, then picks up more materials if needed. Commercial and residential.
- Two painters on staff now; used to be a larger crew. Established firm: good work leads to more work. "If you cheat somebody, the internet is unforgiving. Honesty goes a long way."
- Pitch to customers: "I'm not the cheapest in town, but I will get the job done right." People want peace of mind and trust. Compared with picking up laborers at a home-improvement store ("you might get lucky"), "with me, I promise you good work no matter what."

## Pains

- **Low-bid competition:** he can bid a house at, say, $5K and someone else tells the customer they will do it for $1K, sometimes going behind his back. The contractor in question was going to hire an unlicensed worker at about $35/hr to give him a shot. He attributes much of the undercutting to people willing to work for very little.
- **Materials:** getting materials is an issue; he typically buys the day before.
- **Finding and vetting good people:** needs background checks because some applicants have histories you do not want around. Solid painters get $35-40/hr.
- **How he decides whom to hire:** offers $25/hr to try a person, watches them for the first hour, and keeps checking back "like a hawk." Quick tells: ask what the toughest sheen to paint correctly is (answer: satin); watch how they open a paint can; calling a bucket a "pail" or "pot" signals an old-school painter; cleaning the brush well after the job marks a real painter.
