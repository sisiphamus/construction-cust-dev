# Architect A (large international architecture/engineering firm) - robots will outrun construction labor

Context: the recording also contains the opening of a mall-redevelopment panel at a national architecture convention (unidentified panelists and audience members). Panel themes: extending street grids through dead malls, cities worrying about what happens if a mall disappears, administrative (checklist-based) review so projects that meet intent avoid going back to planning commissions, cheap former-government land, and data centers raising land value. Interviewer 1 recorded the session and approached an audience member who had asked a forward-looking question about drones.

## What the architect said
- Built malls and shopping centers until about 2018, then concluded the retail model was dying and retrained into industrial/advanced-manufacturing architecture.
- Evidence of automation: the same distribution/fulfillment/plant building at the same square footage went from about 1,200 parking stalls to about 700 over roughly six years because about half the jobs were eliminated by robots.
- Her view: the technology is already there; adoption speed is limited by people being slow to accept robots (analogy to robot vacuums), not by robot capability. Humanoid robots will do electrical and other trade work sooner than people expect; anyone designing buildings across healthcare, K-12, residential etc. must account for it.
- Data centers: construction must ramp up within about three years, builds take about 18 months, and construction labor on them is temporary; she expects workers to be replaced by robots afterward.
- Healthcare: lots of work through about 2030 from code-driven seismic upgrades (California), then growth only with population.
- Said US culture favors faster/cheaper/better and she expects regulation to adapt.

## Interviewer 1's side
- Pitched the project: software that scans plans against code exists already; his angle is helping applicants push back on reviewers with arbitrary interpretations by citing how other reviewers in the same office interpreted the same code ("here are 50 cases where your boss interpreted it this way"). Interested in worker housing around data centers.
- Pushed back that construction robots are far from the near term: a layout-marking robotics startup co-founder told him they chose layout because it is the least confined task, and robots struggle in confined spaces; electricians and plumbers do more problem-solving than assembly-line tasks.
- Mentioned a student drone startup for organ/blood delivery facing multi-year regulatory timelines.

## Signals
- No pain voiced about permitting; no buy signal. Useful as context on automation in industrial design and the data-center construction surge.
