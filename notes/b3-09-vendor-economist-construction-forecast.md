# Vendor Economist A (preconstruction software vendor) - construction economics forecast

## Summary
A presentation, not a customer interview. The vendor tracks ~450K active projects and provides preconstruction software. Key findings: macro headwinds (slowing population growth), margin compression, labor shortage focused on the 20-24 age cohort, a mega-project explosion offering 2-3 years of runway, and data center construction peaking 2029 (build-out extends to the mid-2030s). GCs and architects need to pick profitable niches, improve labor productivity, and stay ahead of material cost inflation.

## Macro outlook
- GDP growth of 2.5-3% from 2027 on.
- Population growth fell from 1% (2024) to 0.2% now, attributed to immigration policy; a structural headwind.
- Real disposable income growth ~0.5% this year (normal 1.5-2%), rebound to ~3% next year.
- Interest rates flat; inflation sticky. Consumer spending a shrinking share of GDP growth.
- Employment paradox: headline unemployment 4.3%, but labor force participation only 61%. Long-term unemployment rising. Ages 25-54 at 84% participation (maxed out). 55+ dropped from 40% pre-COVID and did not recover. Ages 20-24 fell from 80% to 71% participation; youth labor is the only pool left to tap.

## Construction-specific trends
- **Project Stress Index** (delayed, bid-delayed or abandoned projects; 2021 baseline = 100): now 90.2, slightly better, but the driver shifted: abandoned projects are now the main driver rather than delays/holds. Recent shocks: tariffs, an overseas conflict, interest-rate volatility.
- **Top categories by dollars:** the data center ecosystem dominates (4 of top 10: data centers, water/sewage, power infrastructure, power generation). Fast-growing overlooked segments YTD: hotels/motels +150%, hospitals/clinics +116%, dams/canals/marine +80%, elementary schools/preschools +15%, shopping +13%. Data centers get headlines; better value may sit in the overlooked segments.
- **Mega projects (>$1B each):** 78% YoY growth in 2025 (~$110B to ~$200B); 2026 already $66B through April vs $37B a year earlier. Now 25% of nonresidential dollars vs 8-11% historically. Mix: 44% commercial (offices), 25% industrial, 25% civil. Landscape consolidating toward mega projects.
- **Data centers:** $7.4B starts in 2022, $80B in 2025; growth forecast then slowing (2027 +13%, 2028 +8%); peak ~2029 at ~$191B, then ~13% decline in 2030 and flattening. Build-out extends into the mid-2030s as timelines stretch. ~20% of nonresidential dollars now. Supporting infrastructure: power +12% (2026), +6% (2027), +5% after.

## Materials, wages, bids (critical)
- Materials +6% YoY; wages +4.5% (above pre-COVID 3%, stuck at a 4%+ floor); bid prices only +3.4%, below both. This pattern has happened 3 times before and each time caused margin compression. If oil (up 60%+) flows into materials, inflation could hit 8-10% (8% peak in 2018 during a tariff + oil shock).
- Contractor strategy: pick profitable niches by subcategory and geography; cannot rely on broad volume; low-margin work loses money.

## Regional outlook (5-year)
West Coast + Mountain +9% next year (recovering from a difficult 2026); Southeast a consistent performer (demographics, data centers, institutions) and the "breadbasket for growth"; Northeast modest; Midwest mixed.

## Labor productivity (the real lever)
With population growth slowing and every cohort except young adults maxed out, availability is done. The only growth lever left is making existing labor more productive (better tools, automation, efficiency).

## Closing thought
Margin compression is the big risk. Contractors who do not actively manage costs and pick high-margin work will see profitability collapse. Labor is the constraint; productivity is the only path forward.
