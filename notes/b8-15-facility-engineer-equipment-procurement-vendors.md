# Facility Engineer A (federal site operations contractor) - equipment procurement, vendor trust, paper-heavy procedures

## What he does

- Develops the equipment side of a special project: spends time with vendors, reads the manuals, writes the maintenance procedure (and sends it to a technical writer), develops the spare-parts list and gets everything organized.
- He types up the maintenance portion by going through each manual and speaking with other laboratories to incorporate their strategies; he uses the equipment before the procedure is fully built out. The team will become subject matter experts as the number of equipment items grows. Mechanics do more of the hands-on work as it scales; he stays to help troubleshoot.
- Each piece of equipment has an assigned engineer who drafts the requirements and the acceptance test. Spent about three weeks at a vendor factory in the Southwest US doing an acceptance test; it failed, so he had to return, develop a new scope, and plan to bring the vendor to the site to learn directly from the team.
- He expects to buy more equipment in the future, including a unit from Europe.

## Pains

- **Vendors without maintenance routines:** often the site buys equipment from vendors that have no specific maintenance routines.
- **Vendor selection and trust:** at his prior employer he saw how equipment is chosen. One US-based vendor met all specs and wanted the business ("a lot of vendors, the site is just another number"). Selection relies on experience built over years ("if you got a Toyota and it works good enough you stick with it forever"). On the government side, trust is very important. One vendor makes great machines but is overwhelmed with work, and they could not even test the unit during a week on site.
- **Fragmented, paper-heavy systems:** hands-on work is moving off paper to mobile, but vendors use Word documents and the site uses many databases and systems (maintenance and asset-management systems). Communication happens through email and is then put into reports; there are document repositories. "Not very efficient, and it's a big task to change."
- **Culture difference:** the research lab relies on people's skills with procedures behind them, while the operations contractor has a top-down, old-manufacturing mindset and wants people to rely on checklists.
