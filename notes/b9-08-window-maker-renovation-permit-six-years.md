# Window Manufacturer Owner A (Houston-area vinyl window maker) - renovation permit took 6 years

## Summary

A short, noisy booth conversation with the owner of a Houston-area vinyl-window manufacturer (formerly a general contractor) who is trying to develop more multifamily business. Interviewer 2 asked about the approval process and its most painful steps.

## Decisions

None.

## Actions

- Owner: have a family member (good with computers) call Interviewer 2 this week.
- Interviewer 2: follow up by email and try to visit the facility in person to see how their processes run.

## Key takeaways

- **A small renovation took six years to permit.** The building he bought was already permitted (built 2010) but he no longer had the original plans, which the city wanted.
- **Septic and drainage re-review drove the delay.** For a ~2,500 sq ft addition (extending a concrete warehouse by about 5 ft), the city demanded a septic permit and asked whether existing septic capacity and surface drainage were adequate, despite him already having his own detention pond and adding extra fire and other required items. His frustration: the city approved the original septic, so why re-litigate it, and how could he produce designs for a building that predated him? "It doesn't even make sense."
- **His business:** started as a GC, moved into manufacturing vinyl windows, now wants more multifamily and commercial work. Said of the interviewers' technology, "that's the technology people need here," and was open to a visit.
