# Vendor CEO A (Canadian land-data SaaS startup) - reseller / commission partnership discussion

Note: this is a partnership and vendor conversation, not a classic buyer interview. Useful mainly as market and go-to-market signal.

## Background of the contact
- Civil engineer with a PhD in construction technology. Ran a university research center for about six years on development approvals: international e-permitting systems, BIM/GIS integration, automated zoning and building code checking.
- Since 2020 runs a venture studio producing built-environment startups (about six so far), including an online building-code app moving into automated compliance checking, and the land-data company he now runs as CEO and major shareholder.
- The land-data product covers every parcel (demographics, real estate, zoning, official plan, building permits, development applications) for roughly 80 municipalities. It also has a conversational AI layer with MCP connectors for parcel-level lead generation.
- Real estate agent for over 20 years; says "everybody just wants leads" and that lead gen is easier to sell than anything else.

## Call 1 (June): intro and pivot
- Interviewer 1 described the prior permitting work: code checking against municipal codes, plus a tool aggregating reviewer-level timelines so developers know "this reviewer took nine months on prior three-story multifamily projects" and can time loans, saving "six figures a month" in interest. Interviewer 1 then described the move to an AI-enabled lead-gen service (sourcing ICP leads, high-volume outreach, delivering demos).
- Contact said the permitting/insights-for-developers query "has come up many times already" and that his company had moved toward the same lead-gen space; he was impressed by how fast the pivot happened versus the ten years it took his group.
- Views: "SaaS is dead... it all comes down to services"; the future is AI-enabled services; the one service everyone says yes to is bringing customers. The real question is delivery strength.
- Two paths offered: (1) service/reseller deal where the interviewers sell one of the studio companies' products into the US for commission; (2) full venture-studio relationship where the studio comes in as a cofounder with major equity (he acknowledged founders dislike this but said the model exists to pass on lessons learned). He wanted to test the first path ("test it as a customer") before the second.
- Interviewer 1 would only work with products he believes in, noted other service prospects (one possible five-figure deal), and said he must consult his cofounder before committing. Next step: intro to the vendor's product manager for a demo; send the onboarding doc.

## Call 2 (early July): product feedback and economics
- Interviewer 1 gave written feedback on four products: they feel like a data catalog rather than a verdict; too many buttons; needs one umbrella signal for outbound. Cited a prominent Houston developer's advice: "just make it simple... if you can do 99% of the work people want the 1% final say." Users want a clear "is it buildable or not, and why" answer. The developer chatbot product had the best ease of use.
- Vendor response: built with a studio mindset (separate products under one umbrella). He sees reverse-query site selection ("find me the best site for this use case") as the premier product because generic AI cannot do it without parcel-level data.
- Commercial model he wants: performance-based only. Any retainer/hybrid means more market comparison and slower process. He wants the sales partner to own the full US pipeline through close because he has only a part-time US salesperson chasing large accounts; "if you solve one piece, you shift the bottleneck; if you solve the whole pipeline I can expand."
- Data economics: full reverse query works only where data is fully ingested (his home province). In the US, a new area needs either a substantial contract or repeatability. He can do ~$200k of data extraction against a ~$50k contract if repeatable. Typical application needs 5-10 datasets, not the full set; some customers need private data digitized into their own instance (a one-time services contract). A foreign-government energy-grid/site-selection project is in discussion in the $400-500K range.
- Interviewer 1 said his sweet spot is mid-size deals around $50k, and that large enterprise client, multiple small clients, or investor money are the three ways to fund a new data region.

## Call 3 (mid July): territory and scope
- Vendor flagged channel conflict: the proposal's numbers looked like reseller economics (owning marketing and the pipeline), and the vendor already runs Ontario sales. He wants no double-touching of the same customer.
- Interviewer 1 preferred the existing market (Ontario) because commission-only on a new US market (Houston, no data aggregated yet) means heavy upfront work and delayed payout. Contact framed Houston as a strategic opportunity: lead the market, bigger US deals, repeat in other jurisdictions; no customer uses more than 5-20% of the database so data can be brought in as needed.
- Interviewer 1 lacked ICP clarity and database visibility; heard "merchant builders" might be a Houston angle. Defined himself as wanting a sales role (product, ICP, market handed over).
- Vendor: needs product docs, ICP docs, pricing, and a formal agreement first; is onboarding in-house sales staff; said immediate sales means Ontario, and business development could come after a few wins.
- Interviewer 1 noted he now runs sales for about 10 clients and is running low on capacity.

## Takeaways for analysis
- Strong validation that parcel/zoning/permit data and "site selection" is wanted; data coverage by municipality is the scaling bottleneck.
- Buyer-side advice: lead with a verdict, not a catalog.
- Vendor will only pay on performance; slow decision cycle frustrated Interviewer 1.
