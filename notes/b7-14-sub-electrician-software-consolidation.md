# Electrical Sub Owner A (small service electrician) - consolidate software, price competition from unqualified rivals

## Tools and software pain

- Everyone at the firm uses Jobber for pricing and quotes, customer CRM and invoices, and Bluebeam for takeoffs (getting the material list together and knowing how much is needed).
- Wish: software should be more user-friendly and put more into one product. Today there are multiple plans, "this one and that one," and then you need to buy another piece of software for each additional function.
- If they wanted scheduling or answering services, or to track how much a job is making or losing and expenses, they would need separate software that does not easily integrate with their current tools. Leaving the current tool means individually moving all clients and data.

## Business model

- Mostly word-of-mouth marketing. Primarily service work, plus remodeling and restoring power to buildings. They do not really work with GCs.
- Strong view on GCs: "GCs aren't very smart... they're taking the most money." Claim: a sub makes about 17% profit at year end while a GC makes around 40-50%. "We are the smarter guys. We don't need anyone else. Nobody is drawing up plans for us." Liability: "it rolls downhill"; when something goes wrong, the GC's insurance sues the sub's insurance.
- Licensing: roughly 9-10 years of schooling and field work, then a master exam with a pass rate of about 17%.

## Selling and trust

- On service calls he sometimes improvises according to the electrical code, or drives off and comes back with parts. Example: a customer reports flickering lights blamed on a bad breaker; on site it is an over-torqued termination point, but the fix may still be a whole new panel.
- Show customers you know what you are talking about so they trust you; give options on how to avoid big costs. "Everything we sell is something the customer needs."
- Customers have no understanding: if you recommend an AFCI breaker without a great explanation, they say "you're just trying to make a buck," then call the guy down the street who says you do not need it or offers to do it for $50. Analogy: like seatbelts; customers do not realize they are in danger.
- Quote spread: he gives a $3,000 quote and someone else says $1,400; both are licensed and insured.
- Competition from large generator-installation companies that use mechanics rather than real electricians, "certified online for $20," with apprentice licenses just issued.
- Differentiation: talk to one customer at a time, build reputation, spread by word of mouth, separate from people who just got a license.
