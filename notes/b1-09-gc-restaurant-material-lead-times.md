# GC Operations Coordinator A (small Houston design-build GC, restaurants) - lead times cascade through subs

## Role and company
- Owner, two project managers, and this person, who runs ordering, accounting, paperwork and inspections. Building restaurants and small commercial; two big jobs running 7-12 months plus several smaller ones; trying to shift from residential toward commercial.
- Marketing is word of mouth and repeat restaurant clients (one wants them for another location and a new concept), plus van wraps, flyers, door hangers, social.

## Daily workflow
- Morning texts from subs (paperwork, materials) and project managers (schedule the fire-line company for final inspection, find a door hinge with specific measurements). Paperwork is mostly inspection certifications (sprinkler, alarm, hood) routed to the fire marshal; missing or unapproved paperwork lands on this person and delays the job.
- Ordering: match the measurement, check lead time; if needed now, pick up; if it can wait a week, ship. Inventory is a manual list; most items are in stock or from big-box stores, special orders are the risk.

## Pains
- Lead times and special orders: subs are sequenced (sub one waits on a part, sub two waits on sub one). Example: a special-order 5/16-inch bronze bar with a lead time into early June threatened an owner deadline, forcing a plan B (spray-paint substitute). The project manager knew but did not flag it in time because he was stretched across two finishing jobs with nit-picky owners; the miss was human overload, not tooling.
- Sub reliability: about six subs per job (electrical, plumbing, HVAC); owner-preferred subs used if specified. Subs say they start early and show at 1 pm. "You have to be the mean person"; telling them their check depends on finishing motivates them; unreliable subs get fired.
- Inspectors (city, fire marshal) work on their own schedule with no leeway, adding delay.

## Estimating and commercial terms
- Bottom-up: collect bids from the usual electrician, plumber and HVAC subs, add materials, labor, a design fee if they designed it, plus profit. Owners typically bid to about four GCs and choose on price and trust.
- Payment about 30% up front, invoice the whole project at completion, owner holds a retainer paid at the end.

## Tools
- Everything on Smartsheet (schedule, tasks, images, comments, checkboxes, order screenshots with delivery dates); loves it. Tried Procore once on a landscaping/park job and could not get the hang of it. "If it's not broke, don't fix it."

## Signals
- No explicit buy signal. Strong material-optimization signal: lead-time and special-order risk is repeated and directly delays jobs. A generic project-management tool is a hard sell here; the wedge is material timing and lead-time flagging. Schedule slippage, estimating and sub assessment all present.
