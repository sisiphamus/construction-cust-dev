# Mixed conference conversations (architects, students) - client indecision, inbound inquiries, AI worries

## Summary

Interviewer 1 and Interviewer 2 (university students in a summer innovation program) held several short interviews with architects and construction professionals at a national architecture conference. They presented software that helps firms handle inbound inquiries (especially nights and weekends when there is no desk staff), email triage, and lead recruitment.

## Conversation 1: Architect A (small New England firm, about 8 people)

- Pain: managing client expectations and keeping clients happy. Timeline is the biggest issue because clients change their minds constantly.
- "Three-month clips" on projects; clients are never fully satisfied and file change orders. Projects are delayed more by client decision paralysis than by construction.
- Runs a small team and spends much of her time on HR, materials and billing checks, and prospect calls.
- Referenced a talk on "paralysis of choice": the internet makes it worse because clients see endless options and stay unsatisfied.

## Conversation 2: Architect B

- What keeps him up at night is AI's capability and its business implications: tools' current capability versus what they will do in a year, and the need to future-proof. Notes that students now use AI for essays routinely.

## Conversation 3: Student A

- Works in pharmaceutical development; uses AI to learn, summarize dense studies, and for patent and competition writing. Stresses fact-checking because accuracy is critical.

## Interviewers' pitch

- Working with Houston developers and architects to automate client-facing work. Problem: multifamily developers and architects receive large volumes of inbound calls and emails with no desk staff at night or on weekends (11pm, midnight), from people deciding whether to rent or lease, who need a response.
- Pilots in progress with Houston developers: after-hours phone inquiry answering, email inbox triage, and lead-recruitment optimization to drive more inbound.
- Some positive reaction to a price point mentioned (buy signal: pay-if-fixed).

## Background voices

- Unnamed professionals from several states. General theme: growth is the biggest pain for small construction and architecture firms, and market ups and downs hit small firms hard.
