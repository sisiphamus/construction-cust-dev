# Electrical Sub A (6-person electrical contractor) - wants one consolidated tool; word-of-mouth growth; low-bid pressure

## Tools and workflow

- Everyone at the firm uses Jobber (pricing and quotes, customer CRM, invoices) and Bluebeam (takeoffs, getting the material list together).
- Wish: make software more user-friendly and put more into one product. Today it is multiple plans, this tool plus that tool, then another purchase for the next function.
- If they wanted scheduling, an answering service, or tracking job profit/loss and expenses, they would need separate software that does not easily integrate with the current stack. Leaving the current system would mean individually moving every client and its data.
- Team: 3 electricians in the field, 3 in the office.

## Business model and go-to-market

- Mostly word-of-mouth marketing. Primarily service work: remodels, and buildings that lose power. They do not really work with GCs.
- On GCs: "Let's be honest... GCs aren't very smart" because they take the most money. He cited roughly 17% year-end profit for a sub versus about 40-50% for a general contractor. "We are the smarter guys. We don't need anyone else. Nobody is drawing up plans for us." Also: "when something goes wrong, GC insurance sues sub insurance."
- Approach: talk to one customer at a time and demonstrate competence; separate yourself from new license-holders who do not know the work. Reputation spreads by word of mouth.

## Pains

- **Customer education and low-bid competition:** "the customers have no understanding at all." If you recommend a safety item (an AFCI breaker, say) without a great explanation, they call the person down the street who says it is not needed or offers to do it for $50. Example: he quotes $3,000 and a competitor says $1,400; both licensed and insured. Customers do not realize they are in danger, "like seatbelts."
- **Underqualified competition:** large companies installing generators using people certified online for about $20 and apprentice licenses issued a week earlier. Hard for real professionals.
- **Credential depth:** the master electrician exam has a roughly 17% pass rate, after 9-10 years of schooling and field work; he still takes classes.
- **Diagnosis and trust:** example of a customer reporting flickering lights blamed on a breaker, but the cause was an over-torqued termination point, so the fix was replacing the whole panel. He shows the customer he knows the subject so they trust him ("everything we sell is something the customer needs") and gives options to avoid big costs.
- Sometimes has to improvise within the electrical code, or drive off and return with parts.

## Buy signal

Curious. Strongest need is a single consolidated field-service platform with good integration and easy data migration.
