# Developer B (veteran Houston multifamily developer) - title report follow-up, scheduling an in-person look

## Summary
Interviewer 2 called the developer to follow up on the title report pain he described at the conference: spending 5-6 hours sorting through exception documents to find which ones actually touch his property. The interviewer said he thinks the scanning can be automated. The developer agreed to show his workflow in person at his office the following week. He tempered expectations slightly, saying the interviewer may end up needing to talk to the title company and to lawyers directly since they may feel capable of handling it themselves, but he opened the door fully to see the problem firsthand.

- **Specific pain:** title report review, scanning lines and cross-checking exception documents to find which ones affect the property. 5-6 hours per report.
- **Interviewer framing:** "I'm looking for a problem. It sounded like you had a problem. I'm just trying to investigate it." Not pitching or selling; wants to see the workflow first.
- **Buy signal (pay-if-fixed):** the developer asked whether, if it becomes a real business, he would get a chance to buy into it ("we're always up to doing good things and making money"). Interviewer said yes.
- **Adjacent users:** title companies and real estate lawyers do this review manually today; he believes the lawyers he deals with may be using a general AI chatbot for part of it but is not sure how. He describes himself as not very tech-proficient.
- **Next step:** in-person walkthrough at his office the following Wednesday at 9am; the interviewer will send a calendar invite.
