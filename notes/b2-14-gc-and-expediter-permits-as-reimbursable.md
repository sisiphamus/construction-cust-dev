# GC / Expediter A (New Orleans commercial GC with expediting firm) - permits as a reimbursable the GC floats

## Summary

A commercial GC (~$10M revenue) who also runs an expediting firm he started after moving back from New York and noticing there were no expediters in New Orleans. The GC takes ~99% of his time; the expediting company is now mainly a lead generator for the GC. Expediting clients are mostly out-of-town retailers and contractors (national retail and mall tenants) who already know what an expediter is. He rarely gets GCs as clients because he competes with them.

## Biggest insight: permits as reimbursable

AIA contract templates classify permits as reimbursable (no markup), so contractors float $5,000-$50,000 in permit fees for 60-90 days at zero markup and zero finance fee. He argues this should be a profit center, at least a markup to cover financing, just like insurance premiums (every contractor marks up general liability). He has tried to change this convention in Louisiana with limited success because the template just says "permits reimbursable."

## Other topics

- **Expediter discovery problem.** People who look for expediters already know they exist; the average person starting a project thinks to find a GC or architect, not an expediter.
- **Permit office turnover.** "You don't necessarily have a guy because the guy you got might be gone next week," limiting relationship-based expediting.
- **What expediters do.** Tedious tracking, follow-up, written communication, occasional in-person trips to city hall. "Can't cure government ineptitude, only document and follow up."
- **Jurisdictional complexity.** City, state fire marshal (life safety + ADA) and historic preservation often conflict; example: a historic building where the fire marshal required an ADA lift but the historic board would not allow it at the front porch. Cannot use the same process across Louisiana parishes.
- **Portals.** Most major jurisdictions have adopted online portals, but rural areas lag badly; the paper-to-portal transition creates new confusion.
- **Business cycles.** Contractor business lags the news by ~6 months: tariffs and interest-rate changes hit GCs when active projects wrap and the pipeline is empty. Ongoing challenges: real estate cycles, speculative development, material and labor costs.
