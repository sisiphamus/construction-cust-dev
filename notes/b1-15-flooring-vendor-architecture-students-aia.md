# Flooring Vendor A (ultra-high-end residential) and architecture students - permitting is the universal complaint

Participants on the interviewer side: Interviewer 1 and Interviewer 2.

## Main signals
- The flooring vendor works on very large luxury homes across several states. When Interviewer 1 described the product as a "personal legal team" for permitting, he responded: "That's actually really important to me. That's one of the biggest complaints I get from every single person I deal with."
- A residential project was supposed to start in March and was still on hold in June because of permitting.
- He said expediters can earn very well pushing permits through.
- He is personally investing in a fourplex near a data center on a major Pacific Northwest river and is working on a roughly 150-home affordable development for data center workers in a small Oregon town. This supported Interviewer 1's hypothesis that worker housing near data centers is a construction bottleneck.
- Broader chat touched data center economics (chip supply, utilities not keeping up, labor shortage) and conference structure; part of a benchmarking panel was also recorded.

## Architecture students
- Three students (community-college and university, West Coast) joined; one is getting a contractor license next month and works in a family-adjacent contracting business. Another had an unusual quantitative background and returned to architecture school. No specific pain or buy signal from them.

## Close
- Group exchanged LinkedIn connections. Interviewer 1's end summary: sat with three architecture students and a flooring vendor who is buying property near a data center and said housing for those workers is a huge problem.
