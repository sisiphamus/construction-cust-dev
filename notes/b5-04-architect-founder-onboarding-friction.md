# Architect-Founder A (schematic-design startup) - opinionated software, hard onboarding (second record)

## Summary
This is a second transcript of the same conversation summarized in b5-03; the substance is the same. Architect-Founder A built the product to automate schematic design for real estate developers and architects working on large multifamily projects: it answers "what can I build on this plot", automating the tedious part of that stage for larger, complex lots. It is powerful because it encodes decades of architecture experience, which is also why it is hard to onboard.

## Key points
- Product is multifamily today (founder's passion and values: US shortage of ~1.5M homes, denser is more sustainable than sprawl); hospitality and education typologies are the next version. Separate platform with Revit export.
- Team: an in-house group of designers, developers, algorithm/AI experts and QA, taught the design and planning process over a couple of years. Own algorithms, not LLMs, in the product; LLMs used internally for writing.
- Grew entirely organically; the founder found out one day it was ranked as a top AI tool for architects.
- Adoption: "a struggle every day". Biggest hurdle is changing users' existing flow. Many users want to "just prompt and get something", which does not work for complex professional projects. Training courses and onboarding exist but are ongoing. Assumptions are baked in that only someone who understands the market and planning challenges can follow.
- Robotics: founder is very excited; sees construction pain (labor shortage, safety) as large enough that robot adoption could be easier than design-software adoption.
- AIA 2026 observations relayed by Interviewer 2: compliance tools (upload plans, flag code issues per municipality) were the most visible category, plus materials estimating and schedule optimization; heavy data-center and mega-project talk ($60B+ on billion-dollar projects); young-labor shortage; temporary housing near data centers; polarized AI adoption with no middle ground; architects open but unsure where to start, contractors and developers largely resistant. About half of sessions had "AI" in the title.
- Interviewer 2's synthesis: adoption is the bottleneck; people need help taking the first step.

## Next steps
None firm. Founder suggested the McKinsey construction-industry reports as the best map of industry problems.
