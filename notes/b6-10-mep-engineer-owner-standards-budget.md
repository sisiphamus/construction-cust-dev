# MEP Engineer A (Texas MEP consultancy) - owner standards, budget overruns, relationship-driven work

## How design flows
- Most owners have multiple builds and know what they like; standards are kept familiar for their maintenance staff (e.g., Houston market favors central chillers outside). Large owners post design standards online; some school districts have longer standards because they do not know what to say and hire consultants to write them, which leads to over-building.
- CSI divisions give everyone a common scheme for specs; owners state guidelines, equipment types (one owner required double-duct for years), and opinions on zoning/systems.
- The architect is head of the contract and distributes packages. Schematic design: basic renderings, MEP gives brief descriptions of anything the owner has opinions on (manufacturers), someone does a rough budget ("we have been there a lot where the wallet isn't big enough"). Design development: sizes, major room locations, routing; owner and maintenance staff review (e.g., K-12 districts want the data room opening to the hallway and separate).
- Some architects control the process well and tell owners no ("you can't have that in the budget"); others do not.

## Budget overruns
- One project's first estimate came back over budget; the owner said "find the money," forcing large redesigns.
- Good estimators build in cushion so price does not climb later; many estimators price a school like an office and miss higher MEP cost. Costs rise once owners see drawings and develop opinions; the person running the project is not the person running the school, so engineers must find the "unsaid things."
- Example: owner with 10 science labs wanted more art rooms; does not understand cost impact of lab changes.

## Tools
- Revit for all new-project documentation (3D); AutoCAD for small projects and renovations. Load calcs in a legacy load program (command-line, rarely used), a commercial HVAC load tool, and GUIs wrapped around the government energy software ("clunky"). Revit could in theory calculate loads, but architects do not model the building for MEP use, and he is "lucky if I get any info" on wall, roof, window construction. Energy code minimums (U, R, shade values) provide a safety factor; owners rarely ask for more.
- Photometrics largely outsourced to lighting reps (an engineer who knows how can catch their mistakes). Spreadsheets for basic calcs; plumbing sizing by hand.
- Layered, overlapping project-management software is annoying; Revit is not backward compatible so all parties must upgrade; GC and schools each use different software and require responses in theirs.

## Getting work
The architecture firm hires the MEP firm; school districts know and trust certain firms. No marketing; reputation lives in personal relationships and disappears when contacts retire (early-retirement bonuses cleared out one district's contacts).
