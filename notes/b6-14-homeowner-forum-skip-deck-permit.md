# Homeowner forum thread - Houston homeowners on skipping deck permits

## The post
A Houston homeowner is about to rebuild an old deck above the usual permit-exempt thresholds (>30 in. high, >150 sqft), has already taken delivery of lumber, and only now asks about permits.

## What the crowd said
- Dominant sentiment: just build it, especially in unincorporated county. "If its unincorporated just shut up and build it. If you're inside city limits that demand a permit, shut up and build it even faster."
- One pro-permit voice pulled permits on a home addition to protect resale: an appraiser will not count unpermitted square footage when you list. He waited about 3 months.
- Tax disincentive (from a Realtor): if you permit, understate project cost, because next year's property-tax appraisal rises by the stated cost.
- Repair or rebuild of an existing deck may need no permit; install fall-protection railing regardless.
- If you do permit it is not trivial: engineered drawings plus plan review, not a walk-in. Stacking material before permitting was called "out of order."
- Weak, satellite-based enforcement: advice to photograph everything (footers with a tape measure) and build fast because one county buys updated aerial photos every ~3 years to catch new structures.

## Takeaways for analysis
- Confirms homeowner confusion about if/when a permit applies, repair versus rebuild ambiguity, and ignorance of engineered-drawing and plan-review requirements.
- Cuts against a homeowner-outreach thesis: the default reaction to permit friction is to dodge it, not pay someone. Disincentives are concrete (tax bump, ~3-month wait, engineering cost) and enforcement is loose. Willingness to pay for help versus skipping is the open question; this thread leans toward skipping.
- Reinforces an earlier read that clueless homeowners are reachable but low-value; acute pay-to-fix pain sits one notch up at owner-builders and small developers.
