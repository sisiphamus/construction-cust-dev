# Preservation Officer A (retired federal agency) - code interpretation disputes are negotiation

## Summary
He caught the interviewers after a code-publisher session where someone in the audience raised arbitrary code interpretation. Key insights:
- **ADA is civil law, not building code.** The Department of Justice can get involved, and even if a code official approves something (e.g. a ramp), a member of the public can appeal on ADA grounds and demand the 5% slope standard.
- **One state set up a state board that can overrule local authority on accessibility disputes** (meets monthly or quarterly).
- **Code disputes are fundamentally negotiation,** and pictures/visuals help most people who are not "visual" by nature.

## Background and process detail
- Role: federal agencies must have a preservation officer under the National Historic Preservation Act. His was front-end, pre-project: assessing whether buildings were historic before work began, then ensuring facilities personnel preserved character-defining features.
- He chaired design review boards (public, advisory, not decisional; they advise the zoning board or building official).
- On pushing back against his own preservation opinions: he would have acquiesced on demolitions in a historic district if the owner agreed to fund community design guidelines. "It's all negotiation."

## Relevance
Code interpretation disputes, appeals process documentation, and the historic-preservation code layer. Interviewers introduced themselves as Rice students; no business card exchanged. Suggested resource: a national historic-preservation nonprofit for dispute case examples.
