# Developer A (fully integrated developer) - order materials early, pick subs on experience

## Process
- Market work (saturation, rent rates, cost) decides where to build. Fully integrated: acquisition, development team, construction management, finance. Uses GCs in several areas and bids every project to a pool (typically ~4 GCs).
- With a legal background and zoning attorneys he knows roughly when a permit will arrive, so everyone works backward from an expected ground-break date: buy land, bid out GCs, lock a contract, and start buying materials before ground-break. Investors want returns, and carrying costs and workers are being paid, so time is money.
- Bid review advice: rather than just comparing four GC totals, examine each component in a GC's estimate (why each sub was chosen, cheap or expensive). Example: a GC had a very cheap concrete sub; asking why led to a good answer (owned by a large firm, five prior buildings together), which built trust.

## Material insights
- Some metal subs buy coil by the hundreds months ahead and store it, then sell and deliver as prices rise; find subs holding coil and beams, who offer better pricing.
- Steel prices spike on policy and tariff anticipation; he buys domestic steel as more reliable while importers are hurt by tariffs.
- When thick beams were short, a structural engineer welded two thinner beams together.
- Roofing, electrical parts and some elevator components come only from overseas; during COVID elevators could not be built. Now he plans for those delays.
- Wood frame (not his product) has price swings; his buildings are block, tilt-wall or insulated metal panels. "It's all about supply." Labor got very expensive while construction volume recently slowed; higher interest rates mean projects pencil out worse.
- Labor-compliance enforcement can shut down sites when a sub brings in problematic workers, even if the developer did not hire them.

## Subs and tools
- Selects subs by personal experience: who is reliable, who is always late despite being cheapest, who botched the last job. Bids are leveled internally (not in a tool).
- Uses Procore: all GCs and architects are on it for RFIs, drawings and photos; he hesitates to use GCs who refuse it. The construction manager is closer to it than the developer.
- Anecdote: a soil supplier delivered contaminated soil; testing caught it and it was replaced. Lesson: the construction manager should have inspected incoming loads and checked the ticket for origin.
- "Everything I'm telling you is from issues I have run into": no elevator parts, no steel, etc.
