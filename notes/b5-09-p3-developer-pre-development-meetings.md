# P3 Developer A (Texas college-city P3 land developer) - pre-development meetings cut review rounds

## Context
Call with both interviewers. The developer's company is the private partner on a ~1,000-acre P3 where the city owns the land. That gives a deep ongoing relationship with city staff and the ability to write their own planned development with flexible uses. They bring city staff in from day one, their main efficiency lever. P3 does not mean special treatment: same formal process, but staff are partners, briefed in advance, with no hidden agendas. The civil engineer is always the first call.

## Process as run
1. Concept/site plan: civil engineer builds a CAD file from base map data (no survey yet); take it to the city's Development Services for a ~30-minute pre-development meeting with planners, engineers, fire and utilities in one room.
2. Preliminary plan: dimensions, utility corridors, neighbors, use designations; surveyor sets metes and bounds if parcels are carved out.
3. Construction drawings (full set).
4. Plat finalizing the land division.
Commercial buildings: architect designs, submits to the commercial building unit for permit review.

## Ping-pong
Plans circulate to all departments, each with a comment window; the civil engineer or architect responds and resubmits. About 2 weeks per cycle. Best case 2 rounds; with no preparation 4-6. Common comments: wrong phasing, wrong survey reference, missing check valve, wrong code year; mostly easy once you know the pattern. Pre-development meetings cut rounds by surfacing big issues before the formal clock starts.

## One-stop shop vs Houston
His city's Development Services handles water, sewer, storm, variances, fire, rezoning, planning commission and council in one building; he says ~80% of questions are answered there. Houston, in his telling, has three separate offices (county, city planning/development, permitting center) that each point you elsewhere; Houston also has no zoning, which changes the process.

## What a sample review report looks like
He sent two real review notifications: one for a preliminary plan (71 lots, ~27 acres) and one for a final plat (12 lots, ~13 acres) in adjacent phases. Each returned "Revisions Required" with comments from five departments (planning, engineering/public works, water, transportation, electric utility). Comments ranged from minor (update a note number, match hydrant location to construction plans) to structural (show a 25-ft sewer easement, add a 15-ft electric easement, designate private streets with HOA maintenance and an access easement). Sequencing dependencies recur: the preliminary plan cannot be approved until a rezoning clears council, and a final plat cannot reach planning commission until the preliminary plan clears. Repeat comments appear when the prior round's fix was not addressed. This is a mid-prep cycle: neither best case nor worst.

## Read
Pre-submission review is the key lever; the rest is downstream. The sample report shows the real format comments arrive in, a useful dataset. No buy signal.
