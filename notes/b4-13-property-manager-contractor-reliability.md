# Property Manager A (larger Houston residential property management firm) - finding reliable, fairly priced contractors

## Business
- Investors buy single- or multi-family homes and hire the firm for day-to-day tenant interaction, rent collection and maintenance. Mostly single-family; some owners did not set out to be investors (especially in suburbs) and turned a home they wanted to move on from into a rental. Investors often buy older properties and renovate.
- "My work is just emails." "We manage people, not properties": liaison between owner and tenant, plus the contractor side: work-order emails, follow-up on leads for vacant units, and calls when Houston appliance, AC or plumbing problems or storm damage arise.
- Leasing: post on major leasing services and the MLS (syndicates to realtors and listing portals), plus the firm's website; he answers questions on the phone and acts as the prospect's agent.

## Contractor pain
- "The contractor world is a mess": they have a crew for a while, then they do not. Larger, consistent firms with ads and visible trucks are much more expensive. The task is finding good contractors at good prices ("diamonds in the rough") who show up on time. "It's a difficult game."
- Every contractor runs their own way: some only take calls, some only text, some email; each has its own billing, scheduling and CRM, and some hand over a handwritten invoice.
- Scale problem: a good contractor who is grinding and cheap gets busy, hires help who communicate worse and do inconsistent work.
- Some vendors have been used forever with consistently good pricing. As a larger firm, contractors come to them; they are found via Google and chatbot visibility. Example: tired of a rekey vendor's bad communication and rising prices, a replacement just showed up at the office with a business card. They review past calls to check vendors.
- Field staff "run around all day"; many are not strong English speakers (communication friction).

## Tools
Uses AppFolio (property management software): tenant portal for billing and maintenance issues, dispatching a vendor, texting tenants, uploading documentation, accounting, and leasing syndication to increase views. Described as very high-tech compared with the contractors.

## Open questions and asks
Where technicians come from and how to find them; any tool managing the vendors; managing the lease. He is open to spreading the word and asked to be connected with a plumber.
