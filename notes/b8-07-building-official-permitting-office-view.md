# City Building Official A (small Texas Gulf Coast city) - permitting from the office side

## Summary

Interviewer 2, an economics student, ran a research interview to understand permitting from the permit-office side, after hearing strong opinions from developers and permit staff. The recording captured only the interviewer's side, so the official's specific answers are absent. What follows are the questions and the framing the interviewer used, which reflect the themes already heard from other sources.

## Topics covered (interviewer side)

- **Cross-municipality differences:** how different the process looks when a developer used to one city comes to this one (different portals, different regulations) and what problems that causes.
- **Common submittal problems:** why submittals get kicked back. The interviewer's takeaway: applicants consider only the documents they are used to submitting and miss others.
- **Ideal process:** what order a ground-up builder should follow, and whether a pre-construction meeting is recommended so they do not run into these issues.
- **New portal:** the city is adopting a new permitting portal. The interviewer asked about the decision process, how many options there were, and whether the official has seen other departments make mistakes choosing software. Interviewer noted some places still use very old software, and that a large Texas city is already incorporating AI.
- **Long planning timelines:** a developer told the interviewers that on larger projects planning can stretch to months or up to a year. Deeds were mentioned as catching many applicants (consistent with the deed and legal-document delays heard elsewhere).
- **Safety vs process:** a senior permit-office official in Houston stressed that permitting yields tax revenue but is mainly about safety. Interviewer asked what in the day-to-day pulls time away from ensuring code compliance (for example, whether well-formatted submittals save time).
- **Inspector understaffing:** Houston reportedly runs at about half its ideal inspector capacity; interviewer asked whether that is common.
- **Office as code education:** construction and development firms said they teach junior engineers by having them go through the submittal and resubmittal cycle, so the permit office ends up functioning as education for their newer engineers. Interviewer expressed respect for that role.

## Relevance

Fragmentation across municipalities, kicked-back submittals from missing documents (a pre-submittal completeness check), deed and legal document gaps, and the office acting as a code educator all point to demand for tools that tell applicants what to submit and how.
