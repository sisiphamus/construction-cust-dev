# Steel-mill scrap inspector A - scrap intake inspection and material tracking

## Role

Raw-materials inspector at a steel mill. Bulk inspection, including flying a drone over the scrapyard and riding around the yard looking for bad material. A bulk terminal operator offloads much of the scrap; the process runs on spreadsheets (Excel). A terminal can have a limited amount of downtime per week, which creates problems.

## Reporting

- He makes a weekly and monthly scrap detail: what is on the ground, how many weeks of each material the mill has, what is on hand versus in transit versus just bought for the upcoming month. His mill had bought a very large volume of scrap (on the order of 100k+ tons) for the next month from a single supplier.
- The report used to take 8 hours; after automation it takes about 35 minutes. For the past two weeks he has been checking the automation because he does not trust it.

## Safety / quality pain

- Biggest issue: non-ferrous material arriving in sealed containers, and keeping track of it and finding it. If a sealed container is not properly opened and goes into a furnace it can blow up, break equipment and kill people.
- Scrap comes from about 50 scrapyards (a lot from Texas), some very large, "and stuff just gets through." They would like to scan everything somehow, but today they see only the top layer with drones and cannot see underneath.
- Piles of 10k-19k tons must be zeroed out every six months; if unsealed material turns up at that point, they do not know how to trace it back to a source.
- Mitigation today: four operators screen trucks and barges as they are offloaded and know what to look for; large fines ($25-30k) on scrapyards have reduced bad loads ("not as bad as it used to be").
