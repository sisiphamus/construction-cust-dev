# Architect A (Los Angeles small architecture firm) - the resubmit loop and the loss of in-person review

## Q&A (asynchronous)
**Biggest delay people struggle with?** "Not anticipating important issues that will come up as part of the process and not diligently resolving all comments received by the City or County before resubmitting plans." Interpretation: the delay driver is the resubmit loop (incomplete responses trigger another rejection round), not missing documents upfront.

**Do relationships with permit office staff speed things up?** "It helps in the sense that you know what they are expecting and how to deliver on that." Interpretation: relationships are calibration, not favoritism; they teach reviewer expectations so submissions are packaged to match.

**If you controlled regulation end to end, what would you change first?** "I would probably bring back the ability to meet people in-person to resolve issues. The move to online reviews post-COVID has brought many benefits, but it's much harder to meet people in real time now to resolve issues. You used to be able to go to the City and meet all the different departments at the same time. It's impossible to do that now." Interpretation: simultaneous in-person access to planning, building, fire and others allowed parallel real-time resolution; now departments work in isolation and the feedback loop is longer.

## Takeaways
1. Time is lost in resubmit loops, not upfront paperwork.
2. Relationships equal calibration and trust.
3. The post-COVID gap is structural: review became sequential and slower.
