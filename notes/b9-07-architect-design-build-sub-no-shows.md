# Architect A (Houston design-build firm) - sub no-shows are a people problem, not software

## Pains

- **Subs not showing up.** Subs, who are their own business owners, agree to a Tuesday morning visit; the architect rearranges the whole schedule around them; they then say "something came up, I'll be out Wednesday." Root cause is miscommunication or thinking it is "no big deal," without understanding that full schedules are built around their appointed time. Cause is also overbooking or a prior job running long, and they do not warn the rest of the team. He sees this as "managing a person... managing expectations... managing consequences," not a software problem.
  - What he does: tells everyone else to be on site at the same time as the sub so the dependency is visible; sends automatic reminders the day of; and has dropped subs for repeated lateness even when the work was good ("you're not doing the work at the appointed time"). He uses a time-management app that works well for the firm's own scheduling.
- **Seasonality:** work is quieter around the holidays; a client may delay final decisions, pushing schedules; they manage this as best they can.
- **Bid estimating:** uses (1) metrics from previous projects (what they estimated vs what the job actually came in at) and (2) requests for bids from the trades. The number is exposed to market elements out of their control: imported materials, erratic market values, tariffs/uncertainty in the news.
- **Weather and materials:** Gulf Coast hurricane season can cost 3-4 weeks of construction time; material costs rise and shortages follow after a hurricane. Labor is locked in (in-house crew), but materials are not. Everything is purchased wholesale; they have discount accounts and local wholesale places but "it doesn't really help too much."

## Business model and lead sources

- Design-build: because the firm is already the architect, it is a natural transition to construction, so they usually get the build first-look without a competitive bid after the design. Competition is on the front end with other architects.
- Jobs come from: (1) referrals, (2) online presence, mostly Google, (3) AI: people ask an AI assistant and it recommends the firm.
- Mentioned that Houston has among the most residential permits in the US and is becoming very competitive.

## AI use

"We absolutely use AI in every single capacity." Not in actual drawing. Used for code research, pre-development work, putting proposals together, infographics, quick renderings, and a little invoicing. Seen as a competitive edge in showing clients what the firm can provide. A prompt-writing tip: take an image you like and ask AI to break it down into a prompt.
