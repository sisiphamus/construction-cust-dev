# City Development Services Director A (North Texas city) - 4.5 review rounds on average

## Context

Part of a permitting research series mapping the most common resubmittal reasons across the Austin-Dallas-Houston corridor for a planned 2026 reference report aimed at developers and engineering firms. The research was prompted by a development firm operating along that corridor that wanted to know what causes projects to be kicked back.

## Key points

- **Own utilities.** The city runs electric, water, wastewater and solid waste, which is unusual. All utility review is integrated into development review (e.g., checking a garbage truck can enter, pick up, and exit). One team coordinates better but reviews slower than cities with private utilities, where developers coordinate with the providers themselves.
- **Big-ticket trip-ups** in the development process: transportation and utilities.
- **Due diligence.** Buyers do a lot of diligence before acquiring land because of the money involved, including checking utility capacity.
- **Pre-app conference.** ~95% of applicants take it (some cities call it a pre-development meeting). It aligns everyone up front but does not directly cut resubmittals; those depend on how clear the city's comments are and how well applicants respond. Interviewer 1 noted Harris County and Houston uptake rates are much lower.
- **Rounds of review.** Averaging ~4.5 total rounds, a little over 3 resubmittals. Applicants resubmit until all comments are resolved. Comments are all over the board: a bike rack in the wrong location, wrong number of sidewalks, anything tied to a code or regulation, sometimes requirements the applicant does not need.
- **Who submits.** Almost always the design professional (an engineering firm, typically a large national engineering/consulting firm), hardly ever the developer. Many repeat customers, but every site is unique (detention location, floodplain relationship, sidewalk width by corridor: a 10 ft sidewalk on one designated corridor vs 5 ft elsewhere), so even repeat engineers get site-specific comments.
- **Technology.** Same portal software for about 26 years; the portal submission has not changed. Changing checklists and piloting a reduced checklist so applicants submit less documentation up front for certain project types.
- **Ask.** He asked what the outcome would be (paper or study) and said a copy would be useful for the city to share.

## Interviewers' pitch on the call

They are collecting codes and requirements and may produce a report of common reasons submittals get sent back, noting another nearby city had just said it is many small things developers should track, and a major city adopted new codes in 2025. A reference engineering firms could consult easily would be the outcome.

## Internal debrief (interviewers, after the call)

- First product step should be a queryable database of codes/requirements.
- This is the first city official who specifically asked for and wants to distribute the output: first warm distribution channel.
- An expediter in another city gives clients a step-by-step checklist and handles ~600 projects/year, yet developers still do not follow it; open question why people with clear checklists and aligned incentives skip the process.
- GC software adoption problem: "so much capability I didn't know where to start."
- A mandatory expediter law in Chicago is a standardization parallel.
- Permitting offices process ~25 sites/day and rely heavily on paper.
- These offices are educational by design but nothing captures "what you should do first."
