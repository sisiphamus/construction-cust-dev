# Vendor Chief Economist A (preconstruction software vendor) - construction economics forecast

A presentation recorded by Interviewer 2, not a two-way customer interview; included for market context.

## Macro outlook
- GDP growth 2.5-3% from 2027. Population growth fell from 1% (2024) to 0.2% now due to immigration policy, a structural headwind. Real disposable income growth 0.5% this year (normal 1.5-2%), rebounding to 3% next year. Interest rates flat; inflation sticky. Consumer spending is a shrinking share of GDP growth.
- Employment paradox: headline unemployment 4.3%, but labor force participation is only ~61%. Long-term unemployment (6+ months) has been rising since 2022. Core workers aged 25-54 are at 84% participation (maxed out). Ages 55+ dropped from 40% pre-COVID and did not recover. Ages 20-24 fell from 80% to 71% (an unexplained drop); youth labor is all that's left to tap.

## Construction-specific trends
- **Project Stress Index** (projects delayed, bid-delayed or abandoned; 2021 = 100): now 90.2, slightly better, but the driver has shifted from delays/holds to abandoned projects. Shocks cited: tariffs, a Middle East war, interest-rate volatility.
- **Top categories by dollars:** the data center ecosystem takes 4 of the top 10 (data centers, water/sewage, power infrastructure, power generation). Fast-growing overlooked categories YTD: hotels and motels +150%, hospitals and clinics +116%, dams/canals/marine +80%, elementary schools +15%, shopping +13%. Better value opportunities exist outside data centers.
- **Mega projects (>$1B each):** +78% YoY in 2025 (~$110B to ~$200B); 2026 already $66B through April vs $37B a year earlier. Now 25% of nonresidential dollars vs 8-11% historically. Mix: 44% commercial, 25% industrial, 25% civil. Implies consolidation toward mega projects.
- **Data centers:** starts of $7.4B (2022) to $80B (2025). Forecast +65% in 2026, +13% in 2027, +8% in 2028, peaking ~2029 at ~$191B, then -13% in 2030 and flat. Build-out extends into the mid-2030s as timelines stretch. About 20% of nonresidential dollars now. Supporting infrastructure: water +3-4%, power +12% (2026), +6% (2027), +5% beyond.

## Costs and margins
- Materials +6% YoY, wages +4.5% (above the pre-COVID 3%, stuck at a 4%+ floor), bid prices only +3.4%. This has happened three times before, each causing margin compression. If oil (up 60%+) flows through, materials inflation could hit 8-10% (as in 2018).
- Contractor strategy: pick profitable niches by subcategory and geography; do not rely on broad volume; low-margin work loses money.

## Regional outlook (5-year)
West Coast and Mountain +9% next year after a difficult 2026; Southeast a consistent performer (demographics and data centers); Northeast modest; Midwest mixed.

## Labor productivity
With every cohort except young adults maxed out, availability is done. The only remaining lever is making existing labor more productive (tools, automation, efficiency).

## Closing
Margin compression is the biggest risk now; labor is the constraint; productivity is the path. The vendor publishes its forecast free and offered the full deck, a 60-minute presentation, a macro book and state-by-state breakdowns.
