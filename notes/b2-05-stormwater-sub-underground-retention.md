# Stormwater Sub A (Houston-area retention installer) - underground retention on multifamily

## Key takeaways

- **Requirement.** Houston-area jurisdictions require underground stormwater retention (or a pond) on multifamily and most large properties, roughly over ~2 acres. Driven by flooding. The system holds rainwater on the property; at its limit it drains to the public storm drain. It is retention only, not water reuse.
- **Cost.** Roughly $40K up to ~$1M depending on property size.
- **Who owns the decision.** By the time the sub is contracted, the civil plans already have the system designed in. The developer/civil engineer specifies it; the sub prices (about a week to produce pricing) and installs. He has not seen a project where it should have been designed in and was not.
- **Timeline.** Newer requirement: he started seeing it ~4-5 years ago; before that it was not mandated and projects mostly used ponds. He frames it as a natural result of the city expanding plus the flooding problem.
- **Enforcement.** The city permitting office. A permit is required before any work; inspection follows. He says inspectors are mostly plumbing-background people (it falls under plumbing) who inspect against what was submitted; he distinguished inspectors from permit reviewers.
- **Rules vary by county.** Rules are fairly standard but hard to keep track of since each county has its own. Runoff to state-maintained roads involves a separate agency.
- **Resubmittal pain (corroborates other interviews).** Interviewer 2 cited a county office where the average project gets ~4 submittals (rejected ~3 times before passing), causing months of delay, with some projects rejected 10-20 times in a row. Interviewer 2 had also spoken with a county department head the day before.
- **Floodplain / drainage reports** were raised by the interviewer as a heavy issue; no detailed answer captured.
