# Architect-Founder A (NYC schematic-design software startup) - adoption is the daily struggle

## Summary
Architect-Founder A is a veteran architect who built, with a math/AI/algorithm team, a platform that automates schematic design and feasibility planning (what can be built on a plot) for large multifamily buildings, currently focused on NYC (Brooklyn, then Manhattan, now the Bronx). Interviewer 2 is a Rice applied-math student exploring the AEC space and had found the product via Google while searching for architectural design software.

## Product and company
- Tool is for developers and architects on large, complex lots; users must be "smart" and understand what they are doing. It automates tedious work in the schematic stage. Standalone platform that exports to Revit.
- Built on the founder's decades of tacit design knowledge, translated into math and algorithms by an in-house team of designers, developers, algorithm/AI experts and QA over a couple of years. They do not use large language models in the product; they built their own algorithms. They do use LLMs internally for writing.
- Grew entirely organically with no marketing spend; the founder was surprised to learn it was being called a "stock AI tool for architects".
- Focus is multifamily by conviction (US housing shortfall ~1.5M units; denser is more sustainable); not doing single-family. Expansion planned to hospitality, education campuses and other metros as users already ask for schools and hospitality.
- Suggested starting reading: the McKinsey construction-industry reports (2017 and later) as a map of industry problems.

## Pain: adoption
- "It's a struggle every day. You need to educate users, and it's very difficult." Continuous onboarding.
- Software is opinionated: it embeds a view of the design process, so users must learn how it "thinks" and change their flow; once they do they save tons of time. Many people today just want to prompt and get something, which does not work for complex professional projects.

## Robotics and AIA conference exchange
- Founder is enthusiastic about construction robotics; thinks adoption could be easier than design software because the labor and safety pain is so large. Interviewer 2 relayed a construction-robotics CEO's point that robots struggle once walls go up, so his robot marks floors before trades arrive, and a Norway example of robots drilling holes overnight.
- From the AIA conference: popular exhibitors were code-compliance tools (upload plans, get flagged against local codes), plus materials estimating and schedule optimization with lead times. Heavy talk of data centers and mega projects (a chief economist cited $60B+ spent on billion-dollar-plus projects), a shortage of 20-24-year-olds entering blue-collar trades, and temporary worker housing plus water/energy/sewer needs near data centers, forecast to taper around 2030.
- AI opinions polarized: from "use it or you're out" to "no AI near my business" (some on environmental grounds). About half the brainstorm sessions had "AI" in the title. Architects are far more open than contractors and developers, who mostly do not want to be near it; architects feel FOMO but do not know where to start. Interviewer 2: adoption, not capability, is the bottleneck; the key is helping people take the first step.
