# Retired Preservation Officer A (former federal preservation officer) - code interpretation is negotiation

## Context

Federal agencies are required under national historic preservation law to have a preservation officer. His role was front-end and pre-project: assess whether buildings were historic before work began, then ensure facilities personnel knew to preserve character-defining features. He chaired design review boards, which are public and advisory, not decisional: they advise the zoning board or building official. Interviewers identified themselves as Rice students (a math student and a physics student) at the conference.

## Key points

- **ADA is civil law (federal justice department), not building code.** Even if a code official approves a ramp, a disabled member of the public can go to the appeals board and demand the standard slope (5%). Approval by the building official does not end the dispute.
- **State override board:** one state set up a board that can overrule local authority on accessibility disputes; it meets monthly or quarterly.
- **Code disputes are fundamentally negotiation.** Pictures and visuals help most people who are not visual by nature. On his own preservation opinions, he said he would have acquiesced on demolitions in a historic district if the owner had agreed to fund community design guidelines: "It's all negotiation."
- **Pain:** inconsistent code interpretation by local officials, and demolition disputes in historic districts that lack community guidelines.
