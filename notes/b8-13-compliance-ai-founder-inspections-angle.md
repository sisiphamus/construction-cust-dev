# Compliance-AI Founder A (code-compliance startup CEO) - deterministic checking, auditable reports, inspections angle

Interviewer 2 attended silently.

## Summary

Founder A is CEO and co-founder of a code-compliance startup. Origin story: she developed an adaptive-reuse project, ran out of money because of permitting delays, and decided to fix the problem. Her team includes a licensed architect with about 20 years of experience. Interviewer 1 reached out after finding the company online and met the team at a national architecture conference.

## Key topics

- **Technical approach:** two structured datasets (regulatory text and 3D design elements) compared deterministically. Not a probabilistic LLM answer. Produces auditable reports showing everything in and out of compliance.
- **Why the "in compliance" report matters:** a risk-averse industry needs assurance, not just a list of problems. Showing what passed is as valuable as flagging what failed.
- **Quality bar:** human QA on all modules before release. "Every error you present in this market is 10x worse than a data point you don't have." Slower to release than the main competing code platform but higher accuracy.
- **Competitor critique:** Interviewer 1's research found the leading code platform maintains accuracy partly through customer bug reports. She agreed this is backwards and dangerous in a trust-critical industry.
- **Reviewer bias / interpretation:** Interviewer 1 described a developer's anecdotes (a reviewer demanding a yellow building, a bus lane for studio apartments). She validated "risk analysis around the interpretive layer": forecasting accurately given reviewer behavior has real financial value (Interviewer 1 cited roughly $150K/month in construction-loan interest for a stalled project).
- **Timeline reliability:** Interviewer 1's framing that developers want reliability more than raw speed (a package-delivery analogy); she agreed.
- **Inspections angle (her suggestion):** compare city-approved plans to as-built site conditions before an inspector shows up, so contractors can self-correct before failing inspection. She sees it as an untapped space requiring computer vision plus ingestion of approved plans, and mentioned a live site-scanning company as a possible partner.
- **IP advice:** file provisional patents on anything built.
- Offered: introductions to her investors and a possible collaboration if the interviewers' summer venture does not work out.

## Relevance

Direct overlap or competitor in code-compliance verification, but constructive. Two usable ideas: reviewer-bias and timeline forecasting as a risk-analysis product, and as-built vs approved-plan comparison for inspections.
