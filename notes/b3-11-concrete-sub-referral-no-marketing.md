# Sub A (New York concrete firm owner) - referral-only growth, owner-direct service, supplier quality slipping

## Takeaways
- Remarkable customer service; the interviewer's read: "Sales isn't getting replaced." Lead-gen play is weak here since he does no marketing and has no need for it.
- Only real pain: for the past two years the ready-mix concrete company he buys from has been skimping and delivering worse concrete (material quality/supply).
- Skeptical of AI replacing the work or the people ("AI can never take the work I do"), embraces a limited set of tech: email, video calls, phones.
- No sign of retirement; expects to be doing the same thing in 10 years.

## How the business runs
- Specializes only in concrete ("a jack of all trades and a master of none"). Tries to do every project quickly and efficiently. Top priority: avoid accidents so everyone gets home.
- About 20-25 workers, one long-tenured team leader plus four other team leaders. Jobs from $5k to $500k. Work day roughly 7:00am to 2:30pm. Busy season May to October; in the Northeast, December to March concrete cannot cure properly, so winter work is mostly demolition and excavation.
- He personally meets the customer, looks at the job, prices it and signs it off. Everyone has his cell number. A young admin handles paperwork. Customers like dealing with the owner; "with a big company you never get to the owner."
- Crew is family-based and referral-hired (guys bring guys); he pays top money and accommodates days off and paperwork help; his first employee has stayed nearly 30 years. He avoids firing and nastiness. Some customers have used him for everything for 30 years.
- If a mistake happens, they rip it out and redo it at no charge; they never disappear.

## Marketing and sales
Zero marketing today. 25-30% of work is referrals and ~80% (overlapping) comes from people seeing his work and asking for his number; it also comes randomly. Early on: business cards and ads in local community newspapers, one job leading to another "like a tree."

## Payment risk
Early on he was burned by customers who never paid and some who bullied him out of payment. Now he screens: if he does not get the right vibe he will not give an estimate, let alone do the work.

## Quotes
- "Always do the right thing... you'll be amazed how it will come back to you. It doesn't cost anything to be kind."
- "Construction is tough work and tough dealing with people."
- "I love my work. I love my employees. I love my customers."
