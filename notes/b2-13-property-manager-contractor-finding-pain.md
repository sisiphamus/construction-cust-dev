# Property Manager A (large Houston property management firm) - finding reliable contractors is a difficult game

## Business

The firm manages single- and multi-family rentals: investors buy the properties and hire the firm for day-to-day tenant interaction, rent collection and maintenance. Mostly single-family. Some owners become accidental investors (moving away but keeping the home), more in suburbs; investors often buy older properties and renovate. The work is "just emails": "we manage people, not properties," acting as liaison between owner, tenant and contractor; handling work orders, dispatching technicians, and following up leasing leads. In Houston, appliance, AC and plumbing issues are constant, and storms add damage.

## Leasing

Listings go to major leasing services and MLS (syndicated to the large portals), plus the firm's website. He answers leads' questions himself and acts like their agent. They filter constantly.

## Contractor pain (core)

- "The contractor world is a mess": they have a crew for a bit and then don't. Large, consistent, advertising contractors are much more expensive (if you see their vans everywhere, prices are high).
- The firm needs good contractors at good prices, "finding diamonds in the rough"; crews that do not show up on time are the problem.
- Every contractor runs their own business: some only take calls, some text, some email; each has their own billing, scheduling and CRM; some hand over handwritten invoices.
- Scaling failure mode: the good solo operator with fair pricing scales, hires help who communicate worse and do inconsistent work.
- Some vendors have been used forever with consistently good pricing. Vendors find the firm because it shows up on Google and AI search; he switched a re-key vendor after bad communication and rising prices when another showed up at the office with a business card. They review past calls before choosing. Many technicians are on the road all day and communication can be difficult.

## Tools

Property-management software (AppFolio) is the core: portals for tenants, contractors and owners; maintenance dispatch and tenant texting; document upload; accounting; leasing support and more listing views. Described as "very high tech" relative to the contractor side.

## Buy signal / asks

Always open to spreading the word. Asked: "Connect me with your plumber." Interviewer questions to explore: where technicians come from and how they are found, and any tools to manage them and leases.
