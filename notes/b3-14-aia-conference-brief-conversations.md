# Conference notes - brief conversations with architects and others

## Summary
Interviewer 1 and Interviewer 2 held several short interviews at the national architects' conference in Houston. Common themes: clients change their minds frequently (paralysis of choice), projects are delayed more by client indecision than construction, and there is interest in AI-driven client communication tools. The interviewers also presented software that helps firms handle inbound inquiries (especially nights and weekends when no desk staff are present), email triage and lead recruitment.

## Conversation 1: Architect A (small New England firm, team of 8)
- Pain: managing client expectations and keeping clients happy. Timeline is the biggest issue because clients change their minds constantly.
- Projects run on roughly "three month clips"; clients are never fully satisfied and file change orders. Projects are delayed more by client decision paralysis than by construction.
- Spends much of the time on HR, materials/billing checks and prospect calls.
- Cited the "paralysis of choice" idea: the internet makes it worse because clients see endless options and stay unsatisfied.

## Conversation 2: Architect B
- What keeps him up at night is AI capability and its business implications: tools' current ability vs what they will do in a year, and the need to future-proof.
- Observed that students now use AI for essays routinely.

## Conversation 3: Student
- Uses AI for learning and for summarizing dense studies in pharmaceutical development; stresses fact-checking because accuracy is critical; also uses it for patent writing and competition content.

## Interviewer pitch (as presented)
- Working with Houston developers and architects to automate client-facing work.
- Problem: multifamily developers/architects get lots of inbound calls and emails with no desk staff at nights and weekends (11pm, midnight) from people researching whether to rent or lease, who need a response.
- Being piloted: phone inquiry answering (nights/weekends), email inbox triage, lead recruitment optimization to drive more inbound. Pilots running with Houston developers. A budget figure was floated and got a positive reaction (the "pay-if-fixed" signal).

## Unidentified professionals (background)
- From the Northeast and Southeast US. General theme: growth is the biggest pain for small construction and architecture firms; market ups and downs hit small firms hard.
