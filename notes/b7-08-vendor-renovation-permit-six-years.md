# Window Manufacturer Owner A (Houston vinyl windows) - renovation permit took 6 years

## Summary

Short, noisy conference-floor conversation with the owner of a Houston vinyl-window manufacturer who started as a general contractor and moved into manufacturing; he is trying to build more multifamily business. His permitting story: he bought a building that was already permitted (built 2010), extended a concrete warehouse by about 5 ft (about 2,500 sq ft added), and the permit took six years. The city asked for a septic permit and the original plans, which he did not have, and wanted to see what was there before: what septic capacity was approved, and whether it and the surface drainage were enough for the added area, even though he already had his own detention pond. His frustration: the city already approved the original septic ("whatever you approve, you approve"), and he could not produce designs for a building that predated him.

He was mildly interested in the interviewers' technology ("that's the technology people need here... permitting, I know, a lot of hassle") and open to a visit.

## Decisions

None.

## Actions

- Owner will have a family member who is good with computers call Interviewer 1 this week.
- Interviewer 1 to follow up by email and visit the facility to see how their processes run.

## Key takeaways

- A small renovation on an existing, already-permitted building took six years to permit.
- Septic and drainage re-review (capacity of existing systems, whether the addition's surface drainage is covered) drove the delay.
- Missing original plans for a bought building became a blocker; the city expected documentation the new owner could not obtain.
- Houston small-commercial renovation pain is a candidate wedge.
