# City Plat Reviewer A (Houston Planning) - replat process walkthrough

## Who

A Houston Planning and Development plat/planning reviewer, a recent architecture graduate, who reviews replats. Single-channel recording; no speaker labels.

## The process (platting / replatting)

Three stages: **check-in, review, recordation.**

**When you need a replat:** to put a use on the land, subdivide it, or change land use. Lots restricted to single-family must be replatted to become commercial.

**Check-in**
- The applicant drafts and uploads all documents on the portal, then submits during the open window: opens Friday 6am, closes Monday 11am, every other week.
- Required: existing survey, title report, the drawing itself, correct GIS coordinates, everything up to date.
- After close, reviewers check completeness. Missing or expired documents or unpaid fees result in an incomplete letter, and the applicant has until the next cycle.
- Deadline: two cycles (about 4 weeks). If nothing comes back or fees are unpaid, the application is inactivated. If fixed Monday or Tuesday right after, it can still be accepted.

**Review** (separate stage)
- A different planner takes over. Assignments go out Wednesday morning; reviews are due end of day the following Tuesday.
- Checks code compliance against the city's subdivision chapter, building lines, typos and details. Example: lots under the 3,500 sq ft city minimum have options (50% building coverage, compensating open space, or an open-space amenities plan if in a floodplain).

**Recordation**
- Applicant books a recordation appointment, pays fees, prints the final plat and brings it to a recordation planner who confirms all review markups were addressed. The city signs and stamps it. Every Wednesday plats go to the county clerk (or the county engineer's office if outside city limits).

## Failure modes

- Number 1 reason for failure: fees not paid (often the client has not paid the applicant, or they forget). Then expired or missing documents (for example a drainage plan that takes a long time to obtain).
- Who fails: small and first-time applicants who do not know the process. Paying fees but missing the four-week window is rare. Restarting has no real downside beyond losing already-paid fees.

## Multi-agency

- More than planning reviews the plat. The county and other agencies leave comments that must all be addressed; if any agency disapproves, the project is deferred even if others approve.

## Load

- Reviewer handles about 12 projects per cycle, all due Wednesday to Tuesday. Most stressful: big cycles with many projects on a fixed clock, and unresponsive applicants whose work rolls into the next cycle.

## Fast path

- Submit Monday, all good: letter that it is checked into the agenda. If nothing is wrong in review you do not hear back until it is recommended for approval at the planning commission the following week (meets Thursdays).

## Relevance

- Supply-side view of the platting leg of the permitting machine. Confirms the recurring theme: the pain is process navigation, document completeness and fee/coordination discipline, and the people who fail are small and infrequent users. Lines up with a first-pass-approval / permitting-as-a-service wedge. Reviewer is an office worker, not a buyer.
