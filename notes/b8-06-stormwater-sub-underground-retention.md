# Stormwater Sub A (Houston drainage subcontractor) - underground retention on Houston multifamily

## Summary

Interviewer 1 spoke with a contractor who installs underground stormwater retention systems for Houston multifamily, at what looks like an industry conference. Audio is in-person and noisy; a few turns are cross-attributed.

## Key takeaways

- **Requirement:** Houston requires underground stormwater retention or a pond on multifamily and most large properties (around 2 acres and up), across the Houston area. The system holds rainwater on the property; once it reaches its limit it drains to the public storm drain. It is not reused or harvested, only retained. Driven by Houston's flooding.
- **Timing:** he began seeing the requirement 4-5 years ago, after about 10 years in the field; previously not mandated, so people mostly built around ponds. He sees it as a natural progression of the city expanding plus worsening floods.
- **Cost:** roughly $40K up to about $1M depending on property size.
- **Who owns the decision:** by the time he is contracted, the civil plans already include the retention system. The developer and civil engineer put it in place; he prices and installs (about a week to produce pricing). In his experience he has never found a project where it should have been designed in but was not. A few underground-system manufacturers are the usual products.
- **Enforcement:** city permitting office. Inspectors are mostly plumbing-background people since it falls under plumbing; they "just go out there and inspect" against what was submitted. He distinguished inspectors from permit reviewers. You cannot do any work without the actual permit; the permit is needed for installation and inspection.
- **Rule changes:** asked if rules change constantly, he said they are fairly standard but hard to keep track of, since each county has its own requirements.
- **Resubmittals (corroborates other interviews):** Interviewer 1 relayed that a Harris County office contact said the average project gets about four submittals (rejected three times before passing), causing months of delay, and some are rejected 10-20 times in a row. Interviewer 1 also mentioned a conversation with the head of a city department the previous day and asked about floodplain and drainage-report issues and about state highway agency runoff rules; answers were not clearly captured.
- A national builder's high-rise project in the region was mentioned in passing (unclear details).
